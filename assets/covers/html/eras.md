# html — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md, and nothing else. The era block of the winner goes into
`cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what follows is the hero paragraph each
era would put in it.

## The need

*Your API needs a page.* Before: JSON only — no layouts, no
components, no browser pipeline. After: `/` with a home page, a root
layout, the core components, error pages, session and CSRF, live
reload — the page a `phx.new` project is born with.

## The register

**Hospitable** — the mechanism receives whoever arrives and has
already laid everything out for them: where there was an API, there
is now a door. Subtitles invite.

Title `HTML`. Subtitle `COME IN`. Badge `GET /` — the route the need
opens. Accent **doorway amber** or the era's nearest, unused on the
shelf. No furniture unless the era's own column has one.

The counts that are true: **one** route, `/`; **one** page; **one**
layout with a root and an app; **six** plugs in the browser pipeline.
The strings a hero may carry at foreground size, one per hero:
`GET /`, `:browser`, `home.html.heex`, `Layouts.app`. Where a wall of
data appears, it is the API; where a door opens in it, it is the page.

## The six heroes

### 1 · Early carton, ~1978-82

A two-colour elevation drawn with a ruler: a long blank wall filling
the frame from 0.20 to 0.75 of the height, its face ruled with rows of
small brackets and braces as a lettering texture — a wall of JSON —
and at 0.55 of the width a single door cut into it, drawn open, a
doormat in plan before it and a path of six flagstones leading to the
bottom edge. Above the door a plate in hand-set grotesque at
foreground size: `GET /`. Flat paper ground; the second spot colour
fills the open doorway. Lockup along the bottom fifth, a lot stamp at
the top left, top right bare paper.

### 2 · Home computer, ~1982-85

A grid floor to a horizon at 0.45 of the height, and standing on it a
tall flat wall with a heavy black outline, 0.70 of the width across
and 0.50 of the height tall, its face patterned with flat rows of
bracket shapes, and a doorway cut in it at 0.55 of the width, open,
filled with flat doorway amber; on the doormat, a small flat blocky
figure seen from behind, one step from the threshold. Slab lettering
`:browser` across the wall's top edge at foreground size. Flat empty
sky above in deep teal. Lockup in slab caps along the bottom, a
hang-tab badge at the top left, the top right the empty sky.

### 3 · Editorial cover, mid-1980s

An even ground of deep teal and one object: a brass door knocker — a
plain ring on a round back-plate — centred at 0.50 of the width and
0.48 of the height, 0.40 of the width across, lit from the upper left,
the plate's edge debossed into the ground and the ring lifted a
finger's width, mid-knock. Engraved on the plate in small serif at
foreground size: `GET /`. Nothing else. Small serif lockup at the
bottom, a review-quote flash at the top left, the top right clear
teal.

### 4 · Console, late 1980s

An airbrushed innkeeper in an open doorway, 0.75 of the height tall
with the head at 0.18 of the height and the centre at 0.45 of the
width, one arm extended in welcome, warm amber light behind from a
room in hard gradients — a table laid, a framed emblem on the far
wall; to the left of the door, the inn's outer wall painted as a
blank surface of small painted brackets fading into the dusk. Painted
lettering `COME IN` on the hanging sign above the door at foreground
size. Extruded lockup across the bottom fifth; a small UPC block at
the top right; the top left quadrant the night sky, calm.

### 5 · CD-ROM, early 1990s

A chrome wall of bevelled data-tiles running across the frame from
0.25 to 0.70 of the height over a perspective grid to a starfield
horizon, and at 0.55 of the width a gateway punched through it,
jambs bevelled, a second brighter grid in doorway amber visible
through it, a lens flare at the lintel's corner; on the near grid, a
path of six chrome tiles to the threshold. Extruded metallic lettering
`HTML` across the lintel at foreground size, rainbow on the bevels.
Chrome lockup along the bottom, a security hologram at the top left,
the top right the starfield.

### 6 · Big box PC, ~1992-96

A great hall seen from its threshold at night in oil: one hearth at
the far end throwing amber light down a long laid table, six chairs a
side detailed into the shadow, a banner above the fire — and, framing
the view, the massive doorway we stand in, its stone jambs carved
with rows of tiny brackets, the door itself swung open to the left
with a carved line on it at foreground size: `Layouts.app`. Extruded
fantasy lettering across the bottom fifth with a hard bevel, lit by
the hearth; a disk-format flash at the top right; the top left the
dark rafters.

## The recommendation

**3, the editorial cover.** The need is a door where there was none,
and the knocker mid-knock is the smallest true picture of it — one
object, lifted, about to be answered — with the route engraved where
a name would be. The wall of JSON the other five draw is the
explanation; the editorial era trusts the reader to know what a
knocker is for.

The shelf as it stands: clustering manual from a home-computer start;
healthcheck2 manual from an editorial start; exdebug console; exdoc
big box; ash big box; coveralls, healthcheck and enhancements starting
over. Editorial has no printed cover; mailer's proposals recommend it
too — two would not be the signal. If it should be one, 1 — the
carton's wall with a door cut in it — carries the *before* the knocker
leaves out.
