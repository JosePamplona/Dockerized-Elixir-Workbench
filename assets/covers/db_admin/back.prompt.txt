Late-1980s console game box cover art. Painted airbrush illustration with
the slightly stiff, heroic look of North American 8-bit era packaging:
hard airbrush gradients, chunky specular highlights, visible matte
cardboard grain.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 5:7 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image. Elixir violet (#4B275F) is the dominant colour
of the illustration.

Back face layout. This face carries no subject: no figure, no scene, no
picture of anything. The face is matte cardboard printed in deep Elixir
violet, one hard airbrush gradient across the whole face, a step
lighter at the upper right and darkening evenly toward the lower left,
with the cardboard's grain visible through the ink. Nothing on the face
is lettered: no title, no caption, no signature, no placeholder text,
no glyphs, no words of any kind anywhere on the face.

Across the middle of the face the printed board continues, bare, and
one embossed shield is blind-stamped into it, centred on the width,
its centre at sixty hundredths of the height, a quarter of the width
wide, in the same violet as the board and barely darker than it — a
watermark rather than a print, and the one mark on this face. Inside
the shield's outline there is one circle, and inside the circle four
small circles, one above, one below, one to the left and one to the
right of its centre, and nothing else. It carries no lettering.

Across the bottom edge, over the full width, a band of the same board
printed in a darker ink, about a fifteenth of the height of the face.

Every mark on the face is printed material or type, and the marks
named above are all of them.
