# versioning — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md (v0.3.0), and nothing else. The era block of the winner goes
into `cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what follows is the hero paragraph
each era would put in it.

## The need

*Your project has a version number, and no versioning: nobody can say
what changed between one build and the next.* Before: `mix.exs` says
`0.1.0` and has said it since the generator wrote it — maybe for years,
maybe in production; what a build carries is in the git log, which
answers no question a reader has. After: a changelog opened at the
version the project is on today — new or well under way — with an
`Unreleased` section to write into as you go, and a line that closes it
as a release.

## The register

**Inaugural** — the mechanism declares that the record begins here,
wherever here is, and keeps it from now on; it claims nothing about
what came before. Subtitles commence (`FROM HERE ON`). Added for this
cartridge: custodial holds what it is handed; inaugural is handed
nothing — there is a long way already behind and none of it written —
and makes the first mark where you stand.

Title `VERSIONING`. Subtitle `FROM HERE ON`. Badge `SEMVER` — the
meaning the number takes, as plain lettering in the era's own type.
Accent **verdigris**, the blue-green of weathered copper, or the era's
nearest, unused on the shelf. No furniture unless the era's own column
has one.

The picture has its shape given by the need: something **already well
under way** — grown, climbed, sailed — with **nothing marked behind
it**, and the **first mark being made now**, at whatever height or
distance now happens to be, with room ahead for the next. The one who
makes the mark is a person, by hand: the changelog is written, not
generated. What must not be drawn is the machine that bumps a number —
that is `--mix-task`, an amenity.

The counts that are true: **three** parts to a version number; **one**
file; **one** `Unreleased` section, always on top; **six** kinds of
change. The strings a hero may carry at foreground size, at most two
per hero: `CHANGELOG`, `UNRELEASED`, and a number. Digits flip below
foreground size, so a number is two characters and a point, large, and
it is `2.3` — not `0.1`, not `1.0`: the mark of something that was
already a long way along when the marking began. None of them is the
cartridge's own version, which is the back's.

## The recommendation

**6, the big box.** The cartridge's one file is a *log*, and the two
claims its papers make about it are that it can be opened at any point
of the voyage and that it is written by hand, for whoever reads it
(DESIGN 3.1, 3.6). A ship's log opened with the land long out of sight
is both claims with nothing invented: the wake is the two years at
`0.1.0`, the unmarked course line is the git log that answers nobody,
the fresh cross is `--init-version`'s default — *here* — and the
person with the quill is the reason the cartridge does not generate
its changelog from commits. The other five each say "first mark, late"
and have to leave the writing out; 3 keeps the book and loses the
voyage, which is the half of the need that is new in v0.3.0.

The shelf as it stands: clustering manual from a home-computer start;
healthcheck2 manual from an editorial start; exdebug console; exdoc
big box; ash big box; version_manager home computer; coveralls,
healthcheck and enhancements starting over. Pending, as recommended:
mailer 3, gettext 1, ecto 4, esbuild 4, tailwind 2, html 3, dashboard
1. A third big box would make it the shelf's most used era, though not
three running — version_manager's came between. If that weighs, 3 is
the yield: the logbook alone, open at its first leaf under
`UNRELEASED`, on an era with one cover made and none from a prompt;
and 1, the pencil tick over a nearly grown head, is the one that says
*at any point of the project's life* fastest, on an era with no cover
at all.

## The choice

**6, the big box** (2026-09-18), as recommended. Writing it into
`cover.prompt.txt` moved three things of the proposal, all for the one
string: a book lying open shows its pages, not its cover, and pages
turned toward a master who faces us read upside down to us. So the
master is seen **from behind his right shoulder**, in lost profile,
and the book reads toward the viewer; `CHANGELOG` is the heading of
the fresh page, in ink, and not a stamp on the cover board; and the
lantern **stands on the table at the right** instead of hanging at the
upper left, which lights the page and the profile from one side and
leaves both top corners to the badge (left) and the seal (right). The
furniture is a bar block at the right edge and not the disk-format
mark, which exdoc's cover already carries.
