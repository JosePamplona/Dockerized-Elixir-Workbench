Late-1980s console game box cover art. Painted airbrush illustration with
the slightly stiff, heroic look of North American 8-bit era packaging:
hard airbrush gradients, chunky specular highlights, visible matte
cardboard grain.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 4:5 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image. The ground of the illustration, edge to edge,
is one tone that reads clearly lighter or darker in value than Elixir
violet (#4B275F).

Hero illustration, painted airbrush: the open door of a vault, set in
a wall of great stone blocks. The ground of the painting, edge to
edge, is that wall: squared blocks of near-black blue-grey stone with
thin darker joints, lit only where the vault's light reaches them.
From the top edge of the image down to a fifth of the height, the wall
runs across the whole width as an even field of the same dark stone,
low in detail, unlit.

In the wall, one round opening, its centre at 0.55 of the width and at
0.47 of the height down, its diameter 0.46 of the width, its rim a
thick ring of riveted steel with a chunky specular highlight along its
upper left. Through the opening the inside of the vault is bright with
warm orange light: a corridor running straight back to a small bright
point at the centre of the opening, both of its walls made of rows of
small steel drawers with brass handles, shrinking with the distance.
Four drawers stand pulled right out of the corridor's walls, two on
the left and two on the right, near the opening, and in each of them a
row of cream index cards stands upright, lit orange. The light spills
out of the opening onto the stone around it in a warm glow that fades
to the dark wall within a tenth of the width.

The vault's door stands swung open to the right, hinged on the right
side of the rim: a round, thick steel door seen at three-quarters, its
inside face turned toward the viewer, 0.30 of the width across, its
centre at 0.86 of the width and at 0.49 of the height down, its edge
showing its great thickness. On its inside face, around a central
spoked wheel handle, four round glass portholes, one above, one below,
one to the left and one to the right of the wheel, each glowing the
same warm orange and holding no mark, with a chunky white specular
highlight on each glass.

In the left foreground stands the developer, seen from behind at
three-quarters: a young woman in a short dark jacket and jeans, her
hair tied back, the top of her head at 0.27 of the height down and her
figure's centre at 0.24 of the width, her figure 0.56 of the height
tall down to her boots at 0.83 of the height down. Her left hand rests
flat on the steel rim at the left side of the opening, and her head is
turned toward the opening, looking in. The orange light from the vault
rims her face, her shoulder and her raised arm; the rest of her is in
dark blue shadow, and her shadow falls long behind her across the
floor toward the viewer.

The floor is flagstones of the same dark stone, lit orange in a
widening band in front of the opening and falling into shadow toward
the bottom edge. The bottom fifth of the image is that floor in deep
shadow, low in detail, and the lockup sits on it.

Title lockup across the lower part of the image, over that shadow:
"DB ADMIN" on one line, set large in heavy chrome-edged capitals with
a hard drop shadow, catching the orange light, centred on the width
and three quarters of the width wide; and beneath it, in smaller
capitals of the same lettering, the subtitle "SEE WHAT YOU KEEP".

The title and the subtitle are all the lettering in the image.

The top fifth of the artwork, across the whole width, is composed as a
quiet area: low detail, no focal element, an even field of tone — the
dark stone of the wall, beyond the reach of the vault's light.

Every mark on the face is printed illustration or type, and the marks
named above are all of them.
