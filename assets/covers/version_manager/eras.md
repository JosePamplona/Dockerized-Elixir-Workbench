# version_manager — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md, and nothing else. The era block of the winner goes into
`cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what follows is the hero paragraph each
era would put in it.

## The need

*You have more than one Elixir project on your machine, and they don't
all want the same Elixir.* Before: one Elixir on the host, the last one
installed; the project from two years ago and this morning's share it,
and switching is something you do by hand, or forget to. After: a file
in the project naming its Erlang and Elixir — you `cd` in and `mix`,
`iex` and the editor get its versions, you `cd` into the next and get
that one's; the file travels with the repository.

## The register

**Fitting** — the mechanism hands each one what was made for it, the
moment they step in, and never the neighbour's. Subtitles assign
(`TO EACH ITS OWN`). Added for this cartridge: hospitable lays the
table for whoever arrives; fitting knows which of several tables is
yours, and changes what is in your hand at the door.

Title `VERSION MANAGER`. Subtitle `TO EACH ITS OWN`. Badge
`ASDF · MISE` — the two managers the file is written for, as plain
lettering in the era's own type, never as marks. Accent **enamel
blue**, or the era's nearest, unused on the shelf. No furniture unless
the era's own column has one.

The picture has its metaphor given: an *elixir* is a flask, a version
is which flask, a project is a room, and `cd` is the step through its
door. What goes wrong without the cartridge is one flask carried into
every room; what changes with it is the right flask in the hand at
each threshold, unasked.

The counts that are true: **two** managers; **two** lines in the file,
Erlang above Elixir; **one** file per project; **one** step to switch.
The strings a hero may carry at foreground size, one per hero:
`.tool-versions`, `cd`, `mise.toml`. Digits flip below foreground
size, so where flasks carry versions they are two characters and a
point, large — `1.14`, `1.17`, `1.19` — and never the full pin. Where
there are doors, each is a project; where there is a hand, it is the
developer's; where a flask is lit, it is the one this room wants.

## The recommendation

**2, the home computer.** The need is *one machine, several
directories, and the switch is a step* — and this era's stock is that
sentence already: the receding grid floor is the one machine, a
free-standing doorframe with no wall around it is a directory, and the
figure half through the frame with the flask taking the frame's colour
is `cd` doing the switching, unasked. Nothing in the picture had to be
invented to explain the cartridge; the other five each need a second
idea — a plan's callout, a tag, a steel arm, a ribbon of light, a
keeper — to say what the doorframe says by standing there.

The shelf as it stands: clustering manual from a home-computer start;
healthcheck2 manual from an editorial start; exdebug console; exdoc
big box; ash big box; coveralls, healthcheck and enhancements starting
over. Pending, as recommended: mailer 3, gettext 1, ecto 4, esbuild 4,
tailwind 2, html 3, dashboard 1. No home-computer cover has been made
from a prompt yet, and tailwind's proposals recommend it too; two is
not the signal. If one of them should move, 5 — the three rings — keeps
the doors and the one lit flask, and the CD-ROM entry has no cover and
no recommendation anywhere on the shelf.

## The choice

**2, the home computer** (2026-09-18), as recommended — the era, not
the hero. The doorframes were written into a prompt and turned down on
reading, before any take: a picture of `cd`, the mechanism, and it said
little. **The hero is the author's**: a shelf of catalogued flasks —
Elixir, violet, above; Erlang, cherry red, below; a version on every
label — and a scientist who has taken one down. That is what a version
manager *is*, several versions kept side by side and the right one
taken, where the six above drew what it *does* at a door; and the two
shelves are the file's two lines, in the two languages' own colours,
with no mark asked for.

What the prompt adds to it, from what the shelf has already cost
(`README.md`, *What the generator gets wrong*): few flasks and large —
three and an empty place above, four below — with short numerals,
since digits flip below foreground size, and the one that must be right
is in the hand (`1.19`); the empty place, so *has taken one* is a body
to count and not a state; an index card in the other hand reading
`.tool-versions`, so the scientist is checking the flask against the
project's recipe and not choosing by hand, which is the *before* of
this cartridge's need; every lettered face squarely to the viewer;
the colours named as things; the top flat cream and the bottom fifth
flat black under the lockup, so the expansion can be made by hand if
the hero's edges come back flat; the era's own furniture, a
hand-written shop price. If the era draws the scientist poorly, 4 and
6 are the two that paint a figure, and 6 was nearly this scene.
