Draw the back face of the box whose front is this picture, in exactly
this printing: the same smooth painted plaster as the walls of the
hall between its arched windows, with the same faint thin lines of
light running across it in straight runs and right-angled turns,
painted the same soft, luminous way — a little lighter toward the upper
middle of the face and darkening evenly toward the edges.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 5:7 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image. Elixir violet (#4B275F) is the dominant colour
of the illustration.

This face carries no subject: no hall, no vault, no windows, no
bookcases, no tables, no lamps, no books, no city, no figure, no
picture of anything. The face is the front's plaster wall, even across
the whole face, painted in deep Elixir violet where the front's is warm
rose: the violet of #4B275F at the middle of the face, deepening to a
violet near black at the four edges, its faint lines of light a pale
lilac, few and far apart. Nothing on the face is lettered: no title, no
caption, no signature, no placeholder text, no glyphs, no words of any
kind anywhere on the face.

Across the middle of the face the wall continues, bare, and one
publisher's colophon is painted into it as a watermark, centred on the
width, its centre at fifty hundredths of the height, a fifth of the
width wide: an upright oval outline, and inside it one open book seen
from the front, its two pages drawn as two curved outlines meeting at
the spine, and nothing else. It is a step darker than the wall around
it, barely visible, a watermark rather than a print, and the one mark
on this face. It carries no lettering.

Across the bottom edge, over the full width, from ninety-three
hundredths of the height to the bottom edge, a band of the same wall
painted a violet near black, with nothing on it.

Every mark on the face is printed material or type, and the marks named
above are all of them.
