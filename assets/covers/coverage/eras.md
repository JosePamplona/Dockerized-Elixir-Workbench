# coverage — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md, and nothing else. The era block of the winner goes into
`cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what follows is the hero paragraph each
era would put in it.

## The need

*You want to know where the tests aren't looking.* Before: a green
suite and no idea which lines it never touched. After: `mix cover` — a
percentage, an HTML report, and a minimum the build can refuse to go
under. Not for: proving the code is right — coverage says where the
tests are, not whether they ask the right questions.

## The register

**Cartographic** — the mechanism charts where you have been, and
leaves the places you have not marked plainly as unvisited; it says
nothing about what is there. Subtitles chart (`WHERE NOBODY LOOKED`).
Added for this cartridge. Its neighbours are not it: *lucid* lays out
what you were handling blind, as it is, where this one knows nothing
of what the blank holds and only draws its edge; *candid* shows what is
happening while it happens, where this one is a record of where you
went; *scrupulous* holds back what you were about to send, and the
gate here is a number agreed beforehand, not a remark.

Title `COVERAGE`. Subtitle `WHERE NOBODY LOOKED` — the design's thesis:
the box does not make the tests better, it makes the gap visible. Badge
`80% MIN` — the minimum `coveralls.json` is written with — as plain
lettering in the era's own type. No furniture unless the era's own
column has one.

The picture's subject is **a charted ground with its blanks**: an area
gone over, most of it, and a few places inside it that nobody went,
shown as plainly as the rest. Three things every hero keeps, because
they are what the need says:

* **Most of it is covered, and not all.** The visited part is the
  larger — about four fifths of the area, the way a healthy project
  stands — and the blanks are few, small and *inside* it, not a
  frontier at the edge: coverage finds the lines nobody touched in
  files everybody thought were tested.
* **A blank is only unvisited.** It is the bare ground, the untouched
  paper, the dark tile — never a hole, a crack, a monster or a fire.
  The box says where the tests are not, not that something is wrong
  there; a blank drawn as a danger is the claim the need's *Not for*
  refuses.
* **The covered is marked by passage, not by quality.** Ink, light,
  footprints, a mower's stripe — something that says *somebody went
  here*, never a tick, a star or a gold finish that says *this is
  good*.

No hero draws code, a terminal, a progress bar, a pie chart or a
screen: the mechanism is not the subject, the ground is. Lettering only
where a slot below asks for it, at foreground size.

## 1 · Early carton, ~1978-82

Hero, two-colour line work on the paper's own tone: the floor plan of a
large building drawn as a technical schematic, seen straight down. The
plan is a rectangle in thin outline from 0.08 to 0.92 of the width and
from 0.22 to 0.78 of the height down, divided by thin walls into twenty
rooms in four rows of five, each with a doorway gap in its walls. A
dashed route in the first ink wanders through the plan from a
doorway in the bottom wall, at 0.50 of the width, entering room after
room. Seventeen rooms are filled with an even flat tint of the second
spot colour, the rooms the route passes through. Three rooms are left
the bare paper of the ground, the route never entering them: the
second room of the top row, the fourth of the second row and the first
of the third row. Below the plan, a scale bar in thin line runs from
0.20 to 0.80 of the width at 0.86 of the height down, ticked in ten
equal steps, the eighth tick drawn longer than the rest. The top fifth
of the image is the plain ground, unmarked.

## 2 · Home computer, ~1982-85

Hero, coarse airbrush over flat hard-edged fields: a floor of square
tiles in perspective, seven across and six deep, from the bottom edge
back to a horizon at 0.54 of the height down, its lines converging at
the centre of the width, every tile edged in heavy black outline. Most
of the tiles glow one flat hot orange, lit from inside; three tiles
stay flat black, unlit — one in the second row from the front at the
second column from the left, one in the fourth row at the centre, one
in the fifth row at the second column from the right. On the front row,
at 0.62 of the width with its feet at 0.88 of the height down, a small
figure 0.18 of the height tall in flat colour and heavy outline walks
away from the viewer, and each tile it has stepped on glows. Above the
horizon the sky is one wide empty field of flat deep blue to the top
edge.

## 3 · Editorial cover, mid-1980s

Hero, one object lit carefully on an even ground: a single sheet of
square-ruled graph paper lying flat, seen straight down, 0.70 of the
width across and 0.62 of the height tall, centred at 0.50 of the width
and 0.52 of the height down, lit softly from the upper left with no
visible source. Its grid is ten squares across and twelve down. Every
square is shaded in by hand in soft graphite, square by square, the
strokes visible, except three squares left the clean white of the
paper: one in the third row at the seventh column, one in the seventh
row at the second column, one in the tenth row at the eighth column.
Beside the sheet, at its lower right corner, one short pencil lies at
an angle, its point toward the sheet, 0.26 of the width long. The
ground around the sheet is one even deep tone edge to edge, the stock's
own texture and nothing else, and the sheet casts one soft close
shadow down and to the right. Nothing else is in the image: no hand,
no eraser, no desk edge. The top fifth and the bottom fifth of the
image are that same even ground, unbroken.

each with a small question mark in ink at its centre. Behind the
explorer, a valley painted in hard gradients falls away to mountains on
the horizon at 0.40 of the height down, under a dawn sky of hard

## 5 · CD-ROM, early 1990s

Hero, chrome and flare over an airbrushed starfield: a planet being

width across centred at 0.50 of the width and 0.54 of the height down.
Most of its surface is rendered solid in saturated colour — oceans,
continents, cloud — and three small patches of it are still bare cyan
wireframe, a lattice of thin glowing lines with nothing inside, set
among the rendered land: one at its upper left, one just below its

## 6 · Big box PC, ~1992-96

Hero, dense oil painting in deep chiaroscuro, one light source: a
cartographer's table at night. A great chart is spread across the
table, filling the image from 0.40 to 0.90 of the height down and edge
to edge, seen from above and a little in front: inked coasts, roads,
forests and towns painted in full detail and carried down into the
browns. Three small regions of the chart, inside the drawn land, are
still bare parchment, each with only its outline inked. A single oil
lamp stands on the chart's upper right, its flame at 0.74 of the width
and 0.42 of the height down, the one light in the room. The
cartographer's hand, from the left edge, holds a pen with its nib
resting at the edge of the largest bare region, at 0.42 of the width
and 0.62 of the height down. Around the table, the study falls into
deep shadow — shelves, rolled charts, a globe — and the top fifth of
the image is that dark, one even deep field across the whole width.

## The recommendation

**1 · Early carton.**

The reason is the box, not the look. A coverage report is a two-ink
document: every line either ran or it did not, and the report prints
the first in one colour and leaves the second the colour of the page.
The carton entry is the only era whose method is exactly that — two
spot colours on bare board, a schematic rather than a scene — so the
picture does not have to *depict* a coverage report; it *is* one, a
plan tinted where the route went and left paper where it did not, with
a scale bar ticked at the eighth of ten steps for the minimum. The
others need a light, a figure or a danger to make a blank read, and
each of those says more than the box does: that the blank is dark, or
remote, or waiting to be conquered. It is none of these. It is only
the room nobody walked into.

The shelf, as the cover record stands: **big box PC** three times
(exdoc, ash, changelog), **console** twice (exdebug, db_admin),
**home computer** once (version_manager), **editorial** once
(test_data), **manual** six times (clustering, health_probe,
dashboard_extras, precommit, test_doubles, credo), the seven base
cartridges on one shared manual front, and **early carton** never.
Carton would be the first.

## The choice

**4 · Console**, chosen against an early-carton recommendation
(2026-09-22); the third console cover after exdebug and db_admin.
Slots for the prompt: title `COVERAGE`, subtitle `WHERE NOBODY
LOOKED`, badge `80% MIN` asked in the hero at the top right, the seal
at the top left over the pale upper sky, no furniture (the era's
column has a half-peeled price sticker, a rental label, a `2ND PRINT`
band and a UPC block; none is asked). What moved from the proposal
into the prompt: the explorer faces the viewer and stands higher, feet
at 0.78 of the height, so the bottom fifth is the ledge's shadow for
the lockup; the dawn sky is pale, for the value contrast with the
board; and the dotted trail on the map goes around the three bare
patches, so the covered land is marked by passage.
