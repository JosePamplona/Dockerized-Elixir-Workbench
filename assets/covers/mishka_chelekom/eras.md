Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md. This box is a special case, decided by the author on
2026-10-07, and it has no six: the era is not chosen from the
repertoire. The library has pictures of its own — the covers of its
blog, <https://mishka.tools/blog>, ten of them, every one a sparrow at
some trade — and the cover is set in that world, so what is proposed
here is three scenes in it, not six eras. Two things were given:

* **The hero is the library's ASCII sparrow**, the one its tasks print,

## The need

*You want ready-made components that are your own code, not a
stylesheet's class names.* Before: every card, timeline and navbar
written by hand, or dressed in class names whose markup and behaviour
are still yours to write. After: `<.card>`, `<.timeline>`,
`<.carousel>` and the rest are function components in the project's
own `components/`, yours to edit — all 74 of them, or the ones named.

And of the version it installs, in the library's own words: *instead
of an agent guessing attribute names or hallucinating an API, it can
now ask Mishka and get the real answer.* Before: an AI tool writes
`<.card>` from memory, and what it writes looks right and does not
compile. After: the tool asks the project's own MCP server which
components there are and what each one takes.

The cover is about the second, by the author's decision: the
cartridge's `--mcp` is an option and off by default, and the picture
is of the box with it on.

## The register

**Wayfinding** — the mechanism knows the way through and says it when
asked: whoever was guessing stops guessing. Subtitles give the answer
(`ASK THE SPARROW`). Added for this cartridge: lucid, db_admin's, lets
you see what you had; wayfinding is about someone else finding it —
the tool that asks.

Title `MISHKA CHELEKOM`. Subtitle `ASK THE SPARROW`. Badge
`74 COMPONENTS` — the unit the design counts in — as plain lettering
in a top corner. No furniture: the world has no commerce in it that
is not a trap.

The picture has its metaphor given by the blog's own cover, turned
round. There the sparrow stands at the start of the maze, frightened,
and the core is far off: the maze is what guessing is. Here the
sparrow is the one who knows it: calm, on its perch, and from the
perch **one** way runs straight through the maze to the core, lit in
the sparrow's amber, where every other corridor is dark steel and red
light. The core is the AI tool; the lit way is the answer it was
given. And the components are on that way: three flat tiles laid in
it like stepping stones, each with a shape of its own.

The counts that are true: **one** sparrow, **one** perch, **one** core,
**one** lit way; **three** tiles with a body, the three pieces the
need names — a card, a timeline, a carousel; **74** only as
lettering, in the badge. The strings a hero may carry at foreground
size: `AI` on the core, as the blog's cover has it, and nothing else
but the lockup and the badge — the blog's cover is a wall of signs,
and this one has none: every screen and plate in it is blank, dark
glass. The sparrow looks at the reader.

Every hero keeps the top two corners calm — the badge takes one and
the seal straddles the other — and its bottom fifth for the lockup:
`MISHKA CHELEKOM` centred at 0.86 of the height, `ASK THE SPARROW`
centred under it at 0.93.

## The recommendation

**1 · The straight way.** The cartridge's two halves are both in it
and each has a body: the three tiles are what it installs, and the lit
corridor is what `--mcp` adds — a way from the AI tool to those very
pieces, through what was guesswork. It is also the blog's own picture
answered: the same maze, the same core, the same sparrow, with the
one thing changed that the release changed. 2 is the same idea with
the tool as the one acting, which is truer to the protocol — the
client asks — but a thin beam is the kind of line a generator
thickens or drops, and the pieces hang small under the bar. 3 is the
plainest and would letter best, and it has no pieces: a cover of the
MCP server alone, for a box that is components first.

The shelf, as the cover record stands: no row is set in a tool's own
world; the nearest is `base`, a brief from outside the repertoire with
the Phoenix bird in it. The era column for this box would read
*borrowed — the world of the library's own blog cover for the version
it installs, described and not reused; the hero its ASCII sparrow*.

## The choice

**1 · The straight way**, as recommended, on 2026-10-07. Its hero
went into `cover.prompt.txt` with three things the proposal left open:
the tiles' widths (0.20, 0.13 and 0.08 of the width, nearest first),
the lettering — the title in heavy condensed capitals of brushed
steel edged with amber, the subtitle in plain amber — and the badge
as a flash of dark glass with cyan capitals in the upper left, the
seal's calm in the upper right.
