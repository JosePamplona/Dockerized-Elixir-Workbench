# Cartridge covers

Every feature of the workbench is a cartridge, and every cartridge can
have a box. This skill is how one gets made. The verdicts, the cover
record and the evidence every rule was drawn from live in
`assets/covers/README.md`; nothing here duplicates them.

## Two parties, and the file between them

**There is no image generator in this session.** Covers are generated by
the user, outside the repository, from a prompt written here. So the
prompt file is the entire handoff, and it has one rule:

> Every prompt and every revision is written to
> `assets/covers/<feature>/cover.prompt.txt` (or `back.prompt.txt`,
> `eras.md`) **in the repository**, before the take that tests
> it. Never to a scratch directory. Hand it over as a path; a diff in
> the reply is a preview of what the file now says, never the thing to
> work from.

A revision that lands somewhere the other party does not read is not a
revision — it is a take spent regenerating the same prompt, and the
identical result then reads as "the fix failed". Two takes went that way
once. When new art arrives, **check `mtime(art) > mtime(prompt)`** before
looking at anything else: a take made from a superseded prompt is not
evidence about the current one.

And when a fix produces *zero* observable change in the thing it
targeted, the first hypothesis is that it never arrived, not that it
failed.

**A hero can be manual.** Some art is made in conversation with the
generator — instructions and its reinterpretations, turn after turn —
and has no single prompt, the way the overlay has none. The image is
then the source, like `overlay.xcf`, and the rule above holds in a
different form: `cover.prompt.txt` opens by saying the hero is manual
and which proposal it started from, and below that it is a **log of
the changes asked of the image**, each written there before the take
that tests it; every turn is the current `art/hero.jpg` plus the last
entry. Everything downstream is the same — the proportion `covers.py
window` names, `pad`, the second turn, `cut`, the invariants, the
counts, the stamping — and the cover record's era column says
*manual* and what it started from, since the six eras are briefs for
prompts and this was not one. What is lost is the recipe: a manual
hero cannot be regenerated, only continued, so it is archived before
anything overwrites it, without exception.

## The invariants

These do not vary, and they are the things a review forgets to look at
because the prompt does not draw attention to them. Check all four on
every take, front or back:

* **The bleed** is quiet, and it is art. The overlay is laid over the
  art, and its board covers the face's margins — on whichever sides the
  design has board; `covers.py window` names them — nothing that has
  to show — hero, lettering, badge — may be in them, and the art runs
  on under them, whole, because the overlay's transparency is not
  promised to be a clean rectangle: whatever shows through, there is
  art behind it (the banner as it is now shows the art above its band). The margins are made by the generator's second turn, extending
  the hero outward; check that they continue the scene and bring
  nothing new into it. (The seal is the one thing meant to sit on the
  join: it straddles the window's corner, half on the board, half on
  the art.) A badge set "bottom left" with no number went under the
  overlay on the first cover made this way, and one set "bottom left of
  the rectangle" went under it on the second.
* **The face** is flat, straight on, filling the frame edge to edge: no
  border, no rounded corner, no drop shadow, no surface behind it, no
  margin of any colour. Not a photograph of a box.
* **The proportions**: the hero is generated at the proportion
  nearest the window's that the generator offers — `covers.py window`
  names it, from the overlay, and the prompt's format block quotes it —
  and the back is 5:7, the whole face; both stated inside the prompt
  and not only as a generator setting. The front's 5:7 is cut by
  `covers.py cut` from the hero's expansion, with the hero in the window.
* **The colour.** The house violet (#4B275F) is the overlay's: the
  printed board around the window carries it on every cover, so the
  front's art does not — its ground is one tone that contrasts in
  value against the violet, so the window reads as a socket holding
  something and not as a hole in the same board, and its accent
  contrasts against both. The back has no overlay, and keeps the
  violet dominant in its own art.

Three plates in a row were reviewed clause by clause while sitting on a
white photographic ground that the format rule forbids by name. Read the
invariants first, then the prompt.

Both blocks go into every prompt **verbatim**, each in the form its
face takes: the format block says the window's proportion for the
front's hero — the one `covers.py window` names — whose image is the
window, and 5:7 for the back, which is the whole face;
the colour block has one form for the front, which sits in the violet
board, and one for the back, which carries the violet itself. Unlike the seal and the overlay, they
generate reliably.

Colour, on the front:

```text
The ground of the composition, edge to edge, is one tone that reads
clearly lighter or darker in value than Elixir violet (#4B275F); the
violet appears in the composition in details, as a secondary colour,
and not as the ground.
```

Colour, on the back:

```text
Elixir violet (#4B275F) as the dominant colour of the composition.
```

Format, for the front's hero:

```text
Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions <RATIO> portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image.
```

`<RATIO>` is the one line of the block that is not verbatim: it is
whatever `./assets/covers/covers.py window` names — the generator's
proportion nearest the window's, read off the overlay as it is at the
time. Nothing else knows that number.

Format, for the back:

```text
Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 5:7 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image.
```

The front's second turn, sent in the same chat with the *padded*
canvas — `covers.py pad` sets the hero in the window of a 5:7 face
with flat grey margins around it, wide where the board is and a sliver
where the generator's proportion falls short of the window's, and the
generator only paints the grey. Its middle clause names what continues on each side, from the
hero:

```text
The flat grey border around this picture is blank. Paint it by
continuing the picture outward into it on every side, to the edges —
<WHAT CONTINUES: the pipe runs on to both edges, the steel-blue ground
continues above and the near-black ground below>. Keep the picture
inside exactly as it is, and add nothing new: no figure, no object, no
lettering.
```

The front used to be one image, 5:7, composed for the whole face with
a bleed sentence in its format block — *the outer margins, the top
sixth of the height, the bottom tenth and a sixth of the width at each
side, are the same even ground, continuing unbroken to the edges* —
so the overlay covered margins the art had kept clear. Three takes on
exdebug showed the generator cannot hold a margin: told the margins
were empty ground it squeezed the hero into the middle of the face,
and told the hero's size in numbers it filled the image to the edge
and the board cut it. Positions of *centres and heights* land;
*extents* and *absences* do not. And the generator has no 5:7. So the
front is two turns now: the hero at the window's proportion — or the
nearest the generator has — which is the window, where the era
composes for the whole image; and the expansion, the same
image extended outward by the generator, so there is congruent art
under the board. The space is made here, not asked for: the generator
keeps its output size, so "extend to 3:4" kept exdebug's hero at full
height and added 40px a side; `covers.py pad` puts the hero in the
window of a 5:7 canvas with grey margins, the generator paints the
grey, and `covers.py cut` finds the hero in what comes back and cuts the
5:7 face around it — the hero exactly in the window, which is
wherever the overlay's design leaves it and no generator places by
itself. Three things about describing space were paid for on
takes and still hold: name no *rectangle*, because the rectangle named
gets drawn at an inset of the generator's choosing; say what a region
*is*, never what it must not hold, because "no rule, no border line"
comes back drawn; and measure every position **from the edges of the
image, in numbers, one per element**.

The block used to open "not a photograph of a physical box, no
perspective, no drop shadow, no surrounding background" — and three
plates in a row came back as a photograph of a box on a white ground.
Its first positive rewrite said the image was *the printed front face
itself, its own printed surface* — and the generator photographed one,
pale edges and a shadow, on clustering's cover. The second said
*illustration, in one plane* — and clustering's first plate came back
a photographed box, bevel, rim and shadow on all four edges. So the
block is the one place with a prohibition in it, in the form the rule
allows: the positive description first, and after it the enumerated
list of what a product photograph would add. The one plate that ever
came clean of the white ground was made with that list. The cover take
was kept: every edge it photographed fell in the bleed, and an overlaid
cover is judged composed, the way a plate is; the plate was not, since
the back has no overlay to hide behind. A hero that brings an edge has
nowhere to hide it — its image is the window — and is retired.

The art is generated **unworn**: no scuff, stain, sheen or crushed
corner. Wear happens to the finished box — board and all — and the box
is finished only after the generator is done with it, so it waits for
a wear pass over the sealed cover that does not exist yet. The era's
*commercial* furniture — a price sticker, a rental label, a second-print
band — is another matter now that the hero is the window: printed on
the cover inside the window it is whole, and it is what makes a
collection look collected. One per cover at most, from the era's own
column in `references/eras.md`, asked for in the hero with its
position; the anachronisms in the other column are still vetoed.

Why each one is fixed, and what a cover may and may not do around them,
is in `references/front.md`.

## The seal and the overlay are never generated

Both are composited by `covers.py stamp`. Asking a generator for the seal
produced four different seals and, once, the word "Nintendo" inside one
— in a prompt that said `No real brand marks or logos`. Asking it for
the band produced a band that was never twice the same and twice in the
accent colour. Never ask for either, and never ask for any real
console's marks, wordmarks or typefaces. The full rule, and how
`covers.py stamp` places them, is in `references/front.md`.

The overlay is `assets/covers/overlay.png`, drawn by hand in
`overlay.xcf`: the house violet board with the workbench's name on it
and a transparent window where the art shows. Its design is its own to
change — it has been a board with four sides, and is now a banner: the
name band across the top, a strip along the bottom, the sides open,
and the art showing above the band. A generated board was the starting
point, but no prompt made it come out as it needed to, so it was
finished in the editor and the XCF is the source; there is no prompt
for it, and it is not regenerated. **The overlay is the one source of
every number about the face.** `covers.py` reads the window off the
PNG — the largest fully transparent rectangle — and takes it from
there; no script, prompt or document repeats its numbers, so redrawing
it is exporting it, and `./assets/covers/covers.py window` says what
the new one leaves: the
window, the sides that have board, the proportion to generate the hero
at. It is scaled to the artwork and laid over it, the way a printer's
overlay goes over art delivered with bleed: the board covers the
margins `covers.py cut` cut around the hero from its expansion, and the
window is the hero — and the art runs on under the board, whole, so
whatever the overlay lets through has art behind it. The board is the one place the violet is guaranteed,
which is why the art under it contrasts with the violet instead of
matching it. The front gets it; the back does not, and carries the
workbench's name its own way — see `references/back.md`.

## Four rules that hold everywhere

**Say what is, never what is not.** "No real brand marks" did not keep
"Nintendo" out of the seal; "not a photograph of a box" came back as a
photograph of a box three plates running; "no rule, no border line"
came back with the rule drawn. A prohibition names the thing, and the
thing named is the thing drawn. Describe what *is* there until nothing
is left ambiguous — the ground continuing to the edge, one flat tone
lit evenly, a cord that is a straight line from post to post and ends
at a post — and the prohibited thing has nowhere to be. The one
prohibition that ever held was the back's list of lettering, scoped to
the face and enumerated, and it held only once the positive
description beside it was complete: a backstop, never the instruction.
The format block carries the other one, for the same reason and in the
same form — see it below.

**Anything named as a container and left unspecified gets filled.** The
same rule from the other side. A corner asked to be clear came back a hole; an unprinted field came back
holding a sigma, then a bottle; a colophon named without its contents
came back holding a monogram. Describe the interior, or ask for calm
rather than emptiness. Worked through in `references/back.md`.

**Numbers land, relations do not.** A stated internal proportion — *5
wide by 7 tall* — came back within 0.02 both times it was asked. A stated
relation between two objects — *the same height*, *twice the width* —
was dropped both times. Give each thing its own measurement.

**One confirming sample is not enough to delete a guess.** It takes a
failure to keep a rule and more than one success to drop one. A guess
deleted after a single clean plate came back true on the next cartridge.


## Making a box, start to finish

The generator is not in the repository, and neither is the judgement. A
box is made by two parties — whoever writes the prompts and whoever
runs the generator and looks at what comes back — and the steps below
say which does what. **Nothing here says what a cover should look
like.** That is what the references and the record are for, and they are
written from the covers that came out wrong, one table row per cover,
with the guesses deleted as they fail. The steps are the same every
time; the verdicts are not, and are not meant to be.

### The front

1. Read the cartridge's own README under
   [`features/<feature>/`](../../../igniter/lib/workbench_igniter/features/).
   The heroes come from what it actually does: the counts, the bodies.
   Read **nothing else about this cartridge**: not its `_archived/`,
   not a previous `eras.md` or prompt, not the cover record's old row.
   A finished hero, read before the six are written, is the hero all
   six turn into.
2. Choose the register, and write **six heroes** — one per era, each
   composed from what that era can do, not one hero restyled six times.
3. Save them as `<feature>/eras.md`, numbered in the repertoire's
   order, and under them **the recommendation**: one number, one reason
   drawn from the cartridge and not from the style, and the era column
   of the cover record as it stands. Nothing is generated at this step;
   [what the file holds](references/eras.md) is there.
4. The user picks. The era comes with the proposal, the choice goes in
   the same file, and the five that lost stay in it.
5. Fill the remaining slots for the winner: title, subtitle, accent,
   badge, furniture, seal corner. The lockup takes the bottom of the
   hero, the badge a top corner, and the seal straddles the other top
   corner, so what the art keeps calm there is a quarter-disc.
6. Assemble the prompt from [the template](references/front.md) — the winning
   era's block now goes in verbatim and in full — and save it as
   `<feature>/cover.prompt.txt`. Every later revision is written to
   that same file, before the take that tests it.
7. Generate it, outside the repository, in two turns. The hero first,
   at the proportion `covers.py window` names, saved untouched — no
   seal, no retouching — as
   `<feature>/art/hero.jpg`. Then `./assets/covers/covers.py pad
   <feature>`, which writes `art/padded.jpg`: the hero in the window of a
   5:7 face, grey margins around it. That canvas goes to the generator
   with the second turn from the foot of the prompt file, which paints
   the grey; the result is saved as `<feature>/art/expanded.jpg`. Then
   `./assets/covers/covers.py cut <feature>`, which finds the hero in it
   and cuts the 5:7 face around it with the hero in the window,
   writing `art/cover.jpg`. When it says the expansion is short on a
   side, the generator cropped or moved the picture: rerun the turn.
8. Check first that the take is answering the current prompt:
   `stat -c %y` on both, and the art must be **newer** than
   `cover.prompt.txt`. A take generated from a superseded prompt is not
   evidence about the current one, and reading it as though it were puts
   a false row in the table below — which is how this step got written.
   The same suspicion applies to the picture: a fix that changed
   *nothing at all* about the thing it targeted usually means it never
   arrived, not that it failed.
9. Look at it against the prompt: every count, every string, every
   position — and then the **invariants**, which the prompt does not
   draw attention to because they never change: the expansion a
   continuation of the hero with nothing new in it, no furniture or
   wear, the flat image filling the frame with no border or background,
   the hero at its proportion and the 5:7 face, the ground contrasting in value with
   the board's violet, a quarter-disc of calm at the seal's corner. Three plates in a row were reviewed
   clause by clause while sitting on a white photographic ground that
   the format block forbids by name. What came back wrong is a row in
   [What the generator gets wrong](../../../assets/covers/README.md#what-the-generator-gets-wrong),
   named after the cover. A take that is wrong on a fact is retired to
   `_archived/art/cover-N.jpg`, its prompt copied beside it as
   `cover-N.prompt.txt`, and *then* regenerated — that order is not
   bookkeeping: the next take is saved to `art/cover.jpg`, the same path,
   so a take not archived before it is a take destroyed by it. A take
   that is wrong to
   the eye of whoever is looking goes the same way, and that eye is not
   something this guide tries to write down.
10. Pick the seal corner from the art, then
    `./assets/covers/covers.py stamp <feature> --corner <c> --badge "<UNIT>"`,
    with `--size`, `--badge-corner` (a top corner, since the lockup
    takes the bottom) or `--lockup` if the art wants them. That lays the overlay over the art, the seal straddling the
    window's corner, the badge inside the window, and writes
    `sealed/cover.jpg`. The badge is asked for in the hero, at a top
    corner, in the era's own lettering; `--badge` typesets one instead
    when the generated one comes back wrong — it used to be the only
    way, because the generator set it at the face's corner and the
    corner was under the overlay, and the corner is in the window now.
11. Fill the row in the [cover record](../../../assets/covers/README.md#cover-record): era, register,
    accent, corner and the options `covers.py stamp` was given.

### The back

Only once the front's row is complete.

1. Write the plate prompt — format verbatim, the era block **cut of
   whatever clause places a subject**, the back layout clause with each
   frame given as a rectangle, the workbench's name as the one lettered
   element in the era's own form and with its own rectangle, the era's
   device named with its contents, the front's accent — save it as
   `<feature>/back.prompt.txt`, generate, and save the plate as
   `<feature>/art/back.jpg`.
   Check the invariants first, as on the front: the name's wording, the
   face filling the frame with no border or background, the 5:7, no
   furniture. Then that the frames end above two fifths of the face, or
   the copy will not fit under them.
2. Measure the plate into `<feature>/back/layout.env`: the frames'
   inner rectangles, the field, the strip, as fractions of the width.
   A scan for the bright chrome pixels along one row and one column
   finds the frames; PIL does it. `PANEL=""` if the
   plate brought its own field, and `ACCENT=` from the record.
3. Write `<feature>/back/copy.md` from the cartridge README, in the
   front's register, one `##` per piece.
4. Take the screenshots from a generated project that has the feature
   installed, each **at its own frame's ratio and at the CSS width its
   own layout needs**: browser pages through
   `playwright-cli` served over local HTTP, terminal output captured
   with a TTY and rendered through `aha`. Save them as
   `<feature>/back/shot-N.png`, 1 the leftmost.
5. `./assets/covers/covers.py back <feature>`. Look at it. Adjust the layout
   or the copy — never the plate — and run it again.
6. Record it: a line in the feature's paragraph of the cover record,
   and whatever the plate did differently in
   [Measured on the backs](../../../assets/covers/README.md#measured-on-the-backs). A plate that has to
   be retired goes to `_archived/art/back-N.jpg` with its prompt, and
   is retired *before* the next one is generated.

## Recording

A take that is wrong on a fact, or wrong to the eye of whoever is
looking, is retired to `<feature>/_archived/art/` **before** the next one
is generated — the replacement is written to the same path and destroys
it otherwise. Covers are `cover-N.jpg`, plates `back-N.jpg`, each on its
own count, numbered at archive time as one more than the highest already
there, never renumbered. The prompt that made it goes beside it as
`cover-N.prompt.txt`. `_archived/` is local and gitignored.

Then write it down in `assets/covers/README.md`: the row in the cover
record, and whatever the take did that the evidence tables do not
already say — as a rule about the generator, never as a description of
the cover. That file is what the next cartridge starts from, and a
hero described there is a hero the next cartridge copies.

**Starting a cartridge over** archives the whole box on its counts:
`art/cover.jpg` and `cover.prompt.txt` as `_archived/art/cover-N`,
`art/back.jpg` and `back.prompt.txt` as `_archived/art/back-N`, and
the `back/` directory entire — copy, shots, layout — as
`_archived/back-N/`, N the plate's number. `sealed/` is emptied, the
record's row is cleared to *starting over*, and `eras.md` is written
fresh from the feature README, without opening any of it.
