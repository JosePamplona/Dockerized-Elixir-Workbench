Mid-1990s PC game cover art. Oil-painted in the fully-rendered style
of the 256-colour era: deep chiaroscuro, dramatic single-source
lighting, a glossy laminated finish.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 5:7 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image. Elixir violet (#4B275F) is the dominant colour
of the illustration.

Back face layout. This face carries no subject: no figure, no scene, no
picture of anything. The face is the printed board in deep Elixir
violet, painted even across the whole face and lit from one source at
the upper right, so the violet is a step lighter there and darkens
evenly toward the lower left, under the glossy laminate. Nothing on
the face is lettered: no title, no caption, no signature, no
placeholder text, no glyphs, no words of any kind anywhere on the face.

Across the middle of the face the printed board continues, bare, and
one round guild seal is ghosted into it, centred on the width, its
centre at sixty hundredths of the height, a quarter of the width
across, in the same violet ink as the board and barely darker than it
— a watermark rather than a print, and the one mark on this face.
Inside the seal's rim there is one anvil seen from the side, drawn in
outline, and nothing else. It carries no lettering.

Across the bottom edge, over the full width, a band of the same board
printed in a darker ink, about a fifteenth of the height of the face.

Every mark on the face is printed material or type, and the marks
named above are all of them.
