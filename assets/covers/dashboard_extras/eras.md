# dashboard_extras — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md, and nothing else. The era block of the winner goes into
`cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what follows is the hero paragraph each
era would put in it.

## The need

*Your dashboard has two pages that show nothing until you install
something: the machine's, and the database's.* Before: OS Data greyed
out with an *Enable* link, Ecto Stats telling you to install a
library; `top` in another terminal, and for the database the catalog
queries you look up every time — which index is never used, who holds
the lock, what is bloated. After: both pages lit: CPU load, memory and
disks; index usage, locks, cache hits, table sizes, long-running
queries — for the database the project is on.

## The register

**Kindling** — the mechanism lights what was already built in and
waiting dark; it brings nothing of its own into the room, only what
the instrument needed to read. Subtitles switch on (`LIGHTS ON`).
Added for this cartridge: candid, the dashboard's, shows what is
happening while it happens; kindling is about the two faces of that
same panel that showed nothing, and the moment they do.

Title `DASHBOARD EXTRAS`. Subtitle `LIGHTS ON`. Badge `2 PAGES` — the
unit the design counts in — as plain lettering in the era's own type.
No furniture unless the era's own column has one.

The picture has its metaphor given, and it is the dashboard's own
word: a *dashboard* is a panel of instruments, a page is an instrument,
and a page that shows nothing is an instrument with its lamp out. What
the box *is* is the two instruments reading — the machine's and the
database's — side by side where the developer already looks; the
switch that lit them is the least of it, and no hero is a picture of a
switch.

The counts that are true: **two** instruments; the machine's reads
**three** things — CPU, memory, disk; the database's is one of
**three** servers — Postgres, MySQL, SQLite — and a hero shows one
store, never three. The strings a hero may carry at foreground size:
`OS DATA`, `ECTO STATS`, and on the machine's instrument `CPU`, `MEM`,
`DISK`. Digits flip below foreground size, so no dial carries numerals:
scales are tick marks, readings are needle positions. Where there is a
person, it is the developer, and they are *reading*, not reaching.
The machine's instrument is always on the left, the database's on the
right.

## The recommendation

**3, the editorial cover.** The cartridge brings nothing of its own —
no page, no option, no code: two instruments that were already in the
panel, and the light they lacked. The editorial entry is the one era
whose whole event is *light on one object*, so the box's single claim
is the picture's single event: a duplex instrument, lit from within,
and nothing else to look at. It also says the merge without a word —
two dials in **one** case is osmon and psql_extras in one box — where
the other five each set two separate things side by side and leave the
"one" to the title. The risk is the era's small type against strings
that have to land: the two nameplates are engraved on the object at
foreground size, not set in the era's type, and the dials carry tick
marks and no numerals.

If the developer has to be in the picture, **4, the console**: the
panel with its owner reading it is the need's *After* drawn literally.

The shelf as it stands: clustering manual from a home-computer start;
healthcheck2 manual from an editorial start; exdebug console; exdoc,
ash and versioning big box; version_manager home computer; coveralls,
healthcheck and enhancements starting over; the base cartridges'
choices pending (mailer and html recommended 3, dashboard and gettext
1, ecto and esbuild 4, tailwind 2). No cover chosen from the six is
editorial yet; a fourth big box would be the signal the repertoire
warns about, which is why 6 — the register's most literal picture, a
lamplighter — is not the recommendation.

## The choice

**3, the editorial cover** (2026-09-18), as recommended. Writing the
prompt moved two things of the proposal, both for reasons already in
the record, and the proposal above is left as it was written:

* **The ground is dark** — one even tone of deep blue-black ink, not
  bone white. The register is *kindling* and the era's whole event is
  light on one object: on a white ground two dials lit from within are
  two cream discs; on a dark one their glow is the picture, and the
  pool of amber under the case is visible at all.
* **The case is matte ivory enamel with black rings, not brushed
  brass.** The record's row on this era (`healthcheck`,
  `healthcheck2`): a metal-and-glass hero overrides the era's finish
  and comes back a glossy modern render, and "the lever is the object,
  or the technique". Both levers are pulled: the object is matte, and
  the hero is asked for as a studio still life reproduced in offset
  with a halftone screen.

The subtitle is set as the era sets it, italic and lower case —
`Lights on`. No furniture: the era's own are all small print, and the
cover's one claim is restraint. Badge top left, seal top right. The
ground is one flat tone to the edges, so the expansion is expected to
be made by hand, as on clustering; the second turn is in the prompt
file for a hero whose edges come back otherwise.

**The hero became manual** the same day: the author took the prompt's
first image through several sessions with the generator, and what came
back is the image in `art/hero.jpg` — the duplex instrument as
proposed, in front of a machine hall of the first computers instead of
an even ground. `cover.prompt.txt` says what it carries, and is its log
from there on.
