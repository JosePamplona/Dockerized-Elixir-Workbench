Early-1980s home computer game cover art. Coarse airbrush over a short,
hard-edged palette that reads as a limited display: wide empty fields
of flat colour, matte board.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 5:7 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image. Elixir violet (#4B275F) is the dominant colour
of the illustration.

Back face layout. This face carries no subject: no figure, no scene, no
picture of anything. The face is matte printed board in flat Elixir
violet, one even tone across the whole face with the faint tooth of
coarse airbrush in it, and the violet runs to the four edges of the
image. Nothing on the face is lettered: no title, no caption, no
signature, no placeholder text, no glyphs, no words of any kind
anywhere on the face.

Across the middle of the face the printed board continues, bare, and
one flat geometric glyph is printed into it in a violet one step darker
than the board, centred on the width, its centre at fifty hundredths of
the height, a quarter of the width across, barely darker than the board
it sits on — a watermark rather than a print, and the one mark on this
face. The glyph is one circle outline, and standing on top of the
circle one short upright rectangle outline, narrower than the circle,
and inside the circle one small square outline, and nothing else. It
carries no lettering.

Across the bottom edge, over the full width, a band of the same board
printed in pure black, the black of a switched-off screen, about a
fifteenth of the height of the face.

Every mark on the face is printed material or type, and the marks
named above are all of them.
