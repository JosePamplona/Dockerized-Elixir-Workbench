# tailwind — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's README and DESIGN.md and nothing else. The
era block of the winner goes into `cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what
follows is the hero paragraph each era would put in it.

## The register

**Comic** — the mechanism is friendly, and a little silly about it. A
layout that was written for Tailwind and styled by an 80 kB snapshot
gets its pipeline back: the classes it was already wearing come to
life, a theme toggle appears with three positions, and a flower-named
plugin brings the buttons. Subtitles endear.

Title `TAILWIND`. Subtitle `DRESSED IN CLASSES`. Badge `CSS/APP.CSS` —
the one source the design states. Accent **daisy pink** or the era's
nearest: the plugin's own colour idea, unused on the shelf. No
furniture unless the era's own column has one.

The counts that are true: **two** sources created; **three**
dependencies (the runner and two icon and component sets); **one**
watcher; **three** theme positions (system, light, dark); **one**
80 kB snapshot replaced; **one** import the build needs from html.
The strings a hero may carry at foreground size, one per hero:
`app.css`, `@import`, `data-theme`, `hero-`, `class=`. Where three
things appear they are the theme's positions; where many small ones,
they are classes.

## The six heroes

### 1 · Early carton, ~1978-82

A two-colour dressmaker's pattern sheet: the outline of a plain shirt
drawn with a ruler at the centre, 0.65 of the width across with its
collar at 0.22 of the height, and pinned to it a dozen small
rectangular tags on ruled leader lines, each tag holding a short
word-shape at small size as a class name, the one nearest the collar
legible at foreground size: `class=`. A sun, a half-moon and a
laptop-shaped glyph drawn small in a row along the top edge at 0.12
of the height as the three theme positions. Flat paper ground; the
second spot colour is the shirt's outline and the tags. Lockup along
the bottom fifth, a lot stamp at the top left, top right bare paper.

### 2 · Home computer, ~1982-85

A grid floor to a horizon at 0.45 of the height, and standing on it a
blocky mannequin with a heavy black outline, 0.65 of the height tall,
centred at 0.50 of the width, airbrushed in flat daisy pink, wearing
a flat cyan shirt onto which small square patches in four flat colours
are flying from the right edge along airbrushed motion lines. On the
floor at the mannequin's feet, a three-position toggle switch drawn as
a slab, set to the middle. Slab lettering `app.css` across the shirt's
chest at foreground size. Empty flat sky above the horizon. Lockup in
slab caps along the bottom, a compatibility strip at the top left, the
top right the empty sky.

### 3 · Editorial cover, mid-1980s

An even ground of daisy pink and one object: a single white daisy,
photographed straight down, centred at 0.50 of the width and 0.48 of
the height, 0.50 of the width across, lit from the upper left so
each petal casts a short soft shadow on the next. At the flower's
centre, where the disc is, a small round three-position switch set in
the yellow, lit the same way. One line of small serif type beneath
the flower at foreground size: `@import`. Small serif lockup at the
bottom, a bookshop price sticker at the top left, the top right clear
pink.

### 4 · Console, late 1980s

An airbrushed tailor, cheerful, 0.75 of the height tall with the head
at 0.18 of the height and the centre at 0.40 of the width, tape
measure round the neck, holding up a finished jacket whose every
panel is a different flat colour with a chunky highlight, and on the
floor beside them a heap of cloth swatches spilling to the right edge,
one swatch flying up with a motion line. On the wall behind at 0.75
of the width, three light switches in a column, the middle one lit.
Painted lettering `data-theme` along the tape measure at foreground
size. Extruded lockup across the bottom fifth; a rental label at the
top right; the top left quadrant the shop's calm wall.

### 5 · CD-ROM, early 1990s

A chrome mannequin torso, 0.55 of the height tall, centred at 0.50 of
the width and 0.45 of the height, over a perspective grid to a
starfield horizon at 0.70 of the height, and wrapping onto it from
the left and right edges two ribbons of bevelled metal in rainbow

shoulder. Extruded metallic lettering `TAILWIND` across the chest at
foreground size. Chrome lockup along the bottom, an award flash at the

### 6 · Big box PC, ~1992-96

A tailor's workshop at night in oil, one lamp at the upper left: a
dress form at the centre, 0.70 of the height tall, wearing a coat of
many small patches, every patch a different cloth painted down into
the shadow with its weave, and on the bench in front at 0.65 of the
height a bolt of cloth unrolling toward the right edge, its selvedge
embroidered with one line at foreground size: `hero-`. Pinned to the
wall behind, three small painted cards — a sun, a moon, a screen.
Extruded fantasy lettering across the bottom fifth with a hard bevel,
lit by the lamp; a `256 COLORS`-idiom flash at the top right; the top
left the lamp's glow and the dark.

## The recommendation

**5, the CD-ROM entry.** Tailwind is a flat markup made to *shine* —
classes that were inert under a snapshot stylesheet lit up by the
pipeline — and the CD-ROM era is the one that can only compose in
chrome and flare. It is also the era whose furniture (hologram, award
flash) is the least silly thing on a comic cover, which keeps the
register from tipping into a cartoon. And the shelf has none.

The shelf as it stands: clustering manual from a home-computer start;
healthcheck2 manual from an editorial start; exdebug console; exdoc
big box; ash big box; coveralls, healthcheck and enhancements starting
over. CD-ROM has no cover; this would be its first. 3, the daisy, is
the runner-up if chrome reads as too much for a stylesheet.
