# credo — the six proposals

## The register

**Tireless** — the mechanism makes the same remark the thousandth time
exactly as it made the first, with no heat in it and nothing to prove,
so nobody has to make it again. Subtitles repeat (`IT NEVER TIRES`).

Read off `NEED.md`, whose first line is the stance: *you want a reviewer
that never gets tired of the same remarks*. The *before* is the same
style argument in every review, by hand, by people who tire and drift;
the *after* is a mark made before the code lands. The need also says
what the register must **not** be: *not for bugs — it reads style and
structure, not behaviour*. So no hunt, no defect found, no alarm. The
stance is repetition without fatigue, not detection.

Two counts are facts and one is not, which matters for what the hero may
be trusted to say. **Five** is a fact: Credo groups its checks by intent
— readability, refactor, warning, consistency, design (`DESIGN.md`,
§Background, off Credo's own `lib/credo/check/`). **One** is a fact: the
box adds one dependency, takes one option, and writes one line in the
hook, `mix credo`. The repeated mark's count below — eleven, nine —
carries no fact at all: it is the rhythm of the register, and it is
composed, not claimed.

## 7 · The author's proposal — the classroom

Not one of the six. The author's, and it is grounded where none of the
six are: in Credo's own first line — "a static code analysis tool for
the Elixir language with **a focus on teaching** and code consistency"
(`deps/credo/README.md:3`). A classroom is not a metaphor laid over the
box; it is the tool's own framing of itself. And it carries both halves
of the register, which the stamp of proposal 3 carries only one of: the
same lesson given to every new class, year after year, without heat —
the remark *and* that it is addressed to whoever arrives.

Composed for era 1, the early carton, because the emphasis is the board
and its lessons rather than a figure, and a blackboard is what
two-colour offset does best: white line work and hand-set grotesque on
one flat field.

An empty classroom, drawn as a two-colour line illustration and filling
the image: a blackboard hung on the back wall, its frame running from
0.10 to 0.90 of the width and from 0.14 to 0.62 of the height, its field
one flat even tone. Chalked on the board in a plain hand at foreground
size, three lines left-aligned at 0.26 of the width, their baselines at
0.26, 0.36 and 0.46 of the height, reading

    [R] Modules should have a @moduledoc tag.
    [W] There should be no calls to `dbg/1`.
    [F] Avoid long quote blocks.

and down the board's left inside edge at 0.15 of the width, a column of
five chalked tags, one character in brackets each, their baselines evenly
spaced from 0.24 to 0.56 of the height, reading top to bottom [C], [D],
[F], [R], [W]. A chalk rail runs along the foot of the board at 0.64 of
the height with one stub of chalk resting on it at 0.30 of the width and
a felt eraser at 0.78, both drawn in line. Below the board, three rows of
empty school desks seen from behind and slightly above, receding, five
desks across in the nearest row, their tops at 0.74, 0.82 and 0.90 of the
height, every seat empty and every desk clear. No figure anywhere in the
image. No file paths, no line numbers, no marks of any other kind on the
board. The bottom eighth of the image is the floor, one flat tone. The
top left corner is the flat wall above the board, an even field of tone.

Furniture, if any: a lot number hand-stamped in ink at the lower right of
the wall.

**The three constraints this hero is written under**, all of them
measured elsewhere and none of them negotiable here:

* **Three lessons, not five, and the categories as letters.** Text below
  foreground size comes back with its characters changed (clustering's
  two small address plates). Five full sentences at foreground size will
  not fit a board that has to leave room for the lockup; five *single
  characters* in brackets are the most reliable lettering there is, and
  they carry the exact fact — `[C] [D] [F] [R] [W]`, where refactor is
  **F** because readability already has the R
  (`lib/credo/cli/output.ex`, `@category_tag_map %{"refactor" => "F"}`).
* **Lessons in a hand, never a terminal.** The three strings are real
  Credo messages, verbatim from its checks, and nothing else is on the
  board. A path and a `12:5` would turn the picture into the mechanism —
  and they are exactly the small text that comes back wrong.
* **The desks are empty.** Occupied, the cover says the developer is
  being schooled, which is not what the box does. Empty, it says the
  lesson is up and stands for whoever arrives — which is what a hook is.

If it goes to era 4 (console) instead, the board stays where it is and a
monumental figure is added at 0.30 of the width with its back to the
room, its head at 0.20 of the height and its feet at 0.88, chalk in its
raised right hand; the three lessons then drop to two, because the figure
takes the width the third needs.

## The recommendation

**3, the editorial cover.**

The reason is the cartridge, not the finish. This is the smallest box on
the shelf that still has something to say: one dependency, one option,
and no configuration file at all — the DESIGN's first decision is that
the box installs the tool *as its author wrote it* and writes no
`.credo.exs`, because a configuration written by the workbench would be
the workbench's taste arriving unasked. A cartridge whose whole content
is one thing, deliberately unadorned, is the case the editorial entry is
for: one object, carefully lit, with space around it and nothing else in
the frame.

And the object is the need itself, not the mechanism. A stamp face worn
perfectly flat and level from use *is* "a reviewer that never gets tired
of the same remarks" — the wear is the tirelessness, and the nine
identical impressions beside it are the remark made again without heat.
Nothing in that picture hunts for anything, which is what `NEED.md`'s
*Not for* asks of this cover: it reads style and structure, not
behaviour.

**Superseded by proposal 7** (the author's, 2026-09-20), and on the
recommendation's own terms: the reason for 3 was that the box is one
thing deliberately unadorned, so the cover should be one object — but 7
is grounded in Credo's own word for itself, *teaching*, which no
proposal here had read, and it carries the whole register instead of
half of it. What survives from 3 is the argument for a **graphic** era
over a painted one, which is why 7 is composed for the carton.

**The shelf, as the era column stands.** Big box PC three times (exdoc,
ash, changelog), console twice (exdebug, db_admin), home computer once
(version_manager). Three of the six have never been carried through as a
chosen era: early carton, editorial cover and CD-ROM — editorial and
CD-ROM have each been a *manual* hero's starting point (health_probe and
dashboard_extras from 3, test_doubles from 5), and early carton has been
the recommendation twice and was chosen neither time (ash and db_admin
both went the other way). So this recommendation is not evening out a
shelf: 3 is what the box argues for, and it happens to be a column the
shelf has never filled.

## The choice

**Proposal 7, era 4 (console, late 1980s), 2026-09-20** — the author's
own hero, in its second era.

It went to era 1 first, the early carton, which 7 had been written for
and which the shelf had never filled. **The carton was abandoned after
one take**, and for a reason rather than a taste: its block carries the
words "a line illustration or **a technical schematic**" verbatim, the
block cannot be rewritten, and the take came back as a dimensioned
technical drawing — twenty-seven position fractions lettered onto the
face with leader rules and the chalk stub captioned "stub"
(`_archived/art/cover-1.jpg`; the measured finding is the row in *What
the generator gets wrong*). A second carton prompt was written with the
fractions cut to eleven and the schematic argued against; the author
stopped it before it was generated and chose the era instead, which is
the cheaper move: one of the two voices asking for a dimensioned drawing
was the era itself, and no prompt outranks a block it has to carry
verbatim.

Console, chosen against a recommendation of 2 (home computer). The
recomposition is not the carton prompt restyled — the era is a brief,
not a costume, and what this one can do is paint with dramatic light. So
the light comes from high on the left, the board is the brightest thing
in the room, the desk and the empty chair stand in the warmer shadow
below it, the ground is a warm ochre airbrushed pale-to-deep, and the
chalk is opaque with a hard specular highlight along each stroke — which
is also what buys the legibility eight lines of chalk need under paint.
The lockup takes the era's own lettering: extruded capitals, hard bevel,
hard drop shadow, in the house violet.

**Hero accepted on take 4 (2026-09-20)**, the second in this era and the
fourth of the box. It is the first take with **no decimal fraction in
its prompt**, and the first to come back with nothing annotated on the
face — the three archived takes and the rule they produced are in *What
the generator gets wrong*. Checked before accepting: the board's frame
begins at 0.190 of the hero, and the mark that is composited along the
window's top was predicted to graze it; stamping the padded canvas
showed it does not, because `pad` mounts this artwork in an 863×1024
window rather than the overlay's own 728×864 and the arithmetic had been
done against the wrong one. The lesson is the cheap one: stamp the
padded canvas and look, instead of computing the clearance.

**Take 5: the mark and the title as one block (2026-09-20).** Take 4 was
expanded, cut and stamped — first with the mark in a tile along the top,
then bare at twice its size, then flanking the title with the seal on
the other side — and the author settled on Credo's mark and the title
**centred together as one block**. A generated lockup cannot give that:
the console genre centres its lockup at the foot whatever it is told,
and a space asked for beside a title comes back filled. So the lockup
was cut out of take 4, which already had it right (`art/lockup.png`,
keyed on its violet fill with a ring for the cream outline and the drop
shadow), take 5 asks for a hero with the lower quarter left as calm
floor and no lettering there, and `covers.py stamp` gained
`--mark-beside-lockup` to set the mark on the lockup's left at its
height and centre the pair. The seal goes back to `tl --inside --size
0.19`. The block tried on take 5's padded canvas: at `--lockup-fit
0.56,0.10` it sat over the desk's legs, at **`0.50,0.05`** it clears them
and stands on the calm floor. The full stamp: `--corner tl --inside
--size 0.19 --mark credo=marks/1-credo.png --lockup art/lockup.png
--lockup-fit 0.50,0.05 --mark-beside-lockup`. Then the author moved the block to the **top**, over the calm ceiling, and the seal to the **foot**, straddling it centred: `--seal-at 0.5,0.93 --mark credo=marks/1-credo.png --lockup art/lockup.png --lockup-fit 0.50,0.05 --lockup-at 0.5,0.13 --mark-beside-lockup` is the sealed face (`--lockup-at` added for it). Take 4 is archived whole: `cover-3.jpg` with its prompt, its
expansion and its sealed face.

**No furniture on this take.** The console column offers a half-peeled
price sticker, a rental label, a `2ND PRINT` band or a UPC block, and
the price sticker is the one mark allowed to carry a real number. It is
left off anyway: two takes in a row drowned in numerals, and the first
clean face is not the place to invite one more. It goes on once a take
comes back without annotations.

It is the third console cover on the shelf (exdebug, db_admin), which is
the era column's own signal — three running is where the rule gets
written. Noted here rather than argued: the first two eras of this box
were chosen on what the picture needed, and this one on what the last
one did.

The hero is generated **from a reference image**: the author's drawing of
a classroom, which goes in `art/reference-classroom.<ext>` and is
attached to turn 1. This is not a manual hero — there is one prompt, in
`cover.prompt.txt`, and it is regenerable as long as the drawing stays
beside it — but the drawing is a source like `overlay.xcf`, so it is kept
and never overwritten. The division of labour is written at the head of
the prompt: the drawing governs the room, the prompt governs the
technique, the board's content, the lettering and the invariants, and the
era block is what stops the generator copying the drawing's own style.

**What the mark costs, said before it is generated.** The era is
two-colour offset on uncoated board, and the mark is composited in full
colour, so on this cover it will be the loudest thing on the face — more
than on db_admin, whose console painting was saturated to begin with.
That is not a reason against it: the seal and the band are composited
too and follow no era, and a box names the tool it installs. But it is a
consequence to see before a generation is spent, and `covers.py` has no
way to lay a mark on in one colour. The name under the square reads
`Credo`, the project's own spelling, while the lockup below reads
`CREDO` — the name twice on one face, which is what a single-mark row
does and what four different admins' names avoided.

**Re-measured against the drawing, 2026-09-20.** The author's classroom
arrived as `art/reference-classroom.png`, 1595×986 — landscape, ratio
1.618, against the hero's 0.8. It is cropped to 4:5 on the board's
centre as `art/reference-classroom-4x5.png`, and *that* is what is
attached: handing the generator a landscape room and a portrait format
is handing it the composition to reinvent, which is the one thing the
reference was for. The original stays beside it as the source.

Every number in the hero paragraph now comes off the crop: the board's
frame at 0.109–0.891 × 0.306–0.593, its field at 0.127–0.873 ×
0.319–0.580, the chair at 0.477–0.601 × 0.577–0.633, the desk at
0.276–0.840 × 0.629–0.913.

Three things the drawing changed in the proposal, and the third is the
best of them:

* **There are no student desks**, and the hero no longer asks for three
  rows of them. The room is board, desk, chair, floor.
* **The five tags are a row, not a column.** The board is 2.3:1 — wide
  and short — so five tags stacked down its left would crowd the three
  lessons. They run along the top of the field, and the lessons sit
  under them without their own tags: a teacher's chalk does not print
  Credo's tag prefix, and the row is the syllabus. Holding the line
  agreed earlier — lessons in a hand, never a terminal.
* **What is empty is the teacher's chair**, not the class. That is
  better than what the proposal had: a room with no students says
  nobody came, and an empty chair behind a board that still carries its
  lesson says the lesson stands whether anyone is there — which is what
  a hook does, and what *never tires* means.

What the prompt takes out of the drawing on purpose, each for a reason
and each listed at the foot of `cover.prompt.txt`: its own chalked line;
the two ceiling fixtures, because the top eighth is where the mark is
composited and where the seal straddles; the map, the flag, the plant
and the side table at the edges, because a country's map and a national
flag put a claim on the box that has nothing to do with the cartridge,
and because the map's state names and the rules poster's six lines are
exactly the small lettering that comes back garbled; and the clock moves
right to 0.72 of the width to clear the mark's lane, its face given
ticks and no numerals for the same reason. All of that is reversible —
it is five lines in one paragraph — but it is what is being generated
now.

**The backticks are dropped from the three messages.** Credo's source
writes them with markup — `` `dbg/1` `` — and chalk has no monospace;
the strings are otherwise verbatim from its checks.
