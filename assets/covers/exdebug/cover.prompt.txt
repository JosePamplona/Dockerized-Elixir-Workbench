Late-1980s console game box cover art. Painted airbrush illustration with
the slightly stiff, heroic look of North American 8-bit era packaging:
hard airbrush gradients, chunky specular highlights, visible matte
cardboard grain.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 4:5 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image.

Hero illustration: a great horizontal pipeline of riveted steel
crossing the whole width of the image at its middle, filling about a
third of the height, airbrushed with hard gradients and one chunky
specular highlight along its top, running off both edges without a
break. The ground is deep steel blue at the top edge, airbrushed down
to near-black at the bottom edge, and the same ground continues behind
everything to all four edges. Set into the pipe, left of centre, one
rectangular sight glass with rounded corners in a bolted steel frame,
twice as wide as it is tall, and floating in its glass, glowing amber,
in bold squared capitals at foreground size, one single line of text
made of exactly these characters in this order: an opening square
bracket, the word Lorem between double quotation marks, a comma, a
space, the word Ipsum between double quotation marks, a closing square
bracket — that is,

    ["Lorem", "Ipsum"]

on one line, with both pairs of quotation marks and the comma drawn.
Standing on a steel catwalk in front of the pipe, to the right of the
sight glass, one technician in grey coveralls and a hard hat painted
Elixir violet (#4B275F), seen from the side in profile, facing left,
as tall as the pipe is wide and a half again: one hand laid on the
pipe with the palm flat against the steel, the other hand at the side,
the face turned to the sight glass, airbrushed in the stiff, heroic
stance of the era — looking, not touching. The sight glass's amber
glow is on the technician's face, on the hand and on the frame, and
fades out on the pipe to either side. Below the catwalk, the
near-black ground with its faint CRT haze, calm, and the catwalk's one
diagonal strut going down into it on the right.

Title lockup across the lower part of the image, over the near-black
ground below the catwalk: "EXDEBUG" set large in a beveled chrome
wordmark with a hard drop shadow, the lettering of the era, centred on
the width and two thirds of the width wide; and beneath it, in smaller
squared capitals, the subtitle "SEEN IN PASSING".

In the top right corner of the image, a small rectangular flash in the
era's lettering, a thin white rule around it on the steel-blue ground,
reading "DEV & TEST" in white capitals at foreground size.

In the bottom left corner of the image, over the near-black ground
beside the title, one round price sticker printed on the cover:
fluorescent orange, its top edge lifting and curling away as if half
peeled, reading "$49.95" in black.

The term in the glass, the title, the subtitle, the flash and the
sticker are all the lettering in the image.

The top left of the artwork is composed as a quiet area: low detail,
no focal element, an even field of the steel-blue ground.

Palette: the ground of the composition, edge to edge, is one tone that
reads clearly lighter or darker in value than Elixir violet (#4B275F);
the violet appears in the composition in details, as a secondary
colour, and not as the ground. CRT phosphor haze in the background.
Every mark on the face is printed illustration or type, and the marks
named above are all of them.
Accent: amber phosphor (#FFB000), the sight glass's glow and the term
in it.
