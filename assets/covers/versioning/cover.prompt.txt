Mid-1990s PC game cover art. Dense oil-painted illustration in the
fully-rendered style of the 256-colour era: deep chiaroscuro, heavy
detail carried down into the shadows, dramatic single-source lighting,
extruded fantasy lettering with a hard bevel, a glossy laminated finish.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 4:5 portrait. Every position below is measured from
the edges of the image. The ground of the illustration, edge to edge,
is one tone that reads clearly lighter or darker in value than Elixir

Hero illustration, dense oil painting: the great cabin of a wooden
sailing ship at night, far out at sea. The ground of the painting,
edge to edge, is the near-black brown of the unlit cabin: dark oak
beams overhead from the top edge of the image down to a tenth of the
height, dark panelled walls at both sides. Across the back wall of the
cabin, one row of stern windows with small leaded panes, its top at a
tenth of the height down and its foot at a third of the height down,
its left end at a quarter of the width in and its right end at three
quarters of the width: through the panes, a moonlit open sea under a
night sky, the horizon a bare straight line at a fifth of the height
down with nothing on it, and the ship's own wake running as one pale
straight band from the foot of the windows back to the horizon at the
centre of the width. One heavy oak table crosses the whole width of
the image, its far edge at half the height down and its near edge at
seven tenths of the height down. On the table at the right, at five
sixths of the width and with its flame at two fifths of the height
down, stands one brass lantern with a single candle flame: the single
source of light in the painting, warm gold, everything falling off
from it into brown shadow. The ship's master sits at the near side of
the table at the left, seen from behind his right shoulder: a
weathered, grey-bearded man in a dark salt-stained sea coat, his head
turned in lost profile toward the lantern so its light rims his brow,
cheekbone and beard, the top of his head at a third of the height down
and his head's centre at a quarter of the width; his back and
shoulder, dark against the light, fill the lower left of the image
down to the bottom edge. His right arm reaches forward across the
table, and the hand, at three fifths of the height down and at half
the width, holds a white goose quill with its nib touching paper. Under
the quill, lying open flat on the table and turned so that its pages
read toward the viewer, one great logbook, half of the width of the
image across, its centre at three fifths of the width and at three
fifths of the height down: the book is open at its very first leaf —
on the left, only the inside of the front cover board, lined with
marbled paper; on the right, the whole thickness of the unused pages
stacked under one fresh cream page. Around the pages the cover shows
as a border of verdigris leather, the blue-green of weathered copper,
with worn brass corners, and one verdigris ribbon marker runs out from
the foot of the spine across the oak. At the head of the right-hand
page, written large by hand in black iron-gall ink, upright capitals
at foreground size, one word:

    CHANGELOG

and under it one single short line of small handwriting as a texture,
the quill's nib resting at the end of that line; every ruled line
below it on the page is empty. Beyond the book, on the far side of the
table between the book and the lantern, one sea chart lies unrolled,
held flat by a pair of brass dividers: one long inked course line
crosses it from its far edge, plain and unmarked along all its length,
and at its near end sits one small fresh cross in the same ink. Every
rope-end, rivet, pane of glass and grain of the oak is detailed down
into the shadows. The bottom fifth of the image is the dark front of
the table's near side and the master's coat in deep shadow, low in
detail, and the lockup sits on it.

Title lockup across the lower part of the image, over that shadow:

In the top left corner of the image, a small rectangular flash in the

At the right edge of the image, at three quarters of the height down,

The word on the page, the title, the subtitle and the flash are all

The top right corner of the artwork is composed as a quiet area: low
detail, no focal element, an even field of tone — the near-black brown
