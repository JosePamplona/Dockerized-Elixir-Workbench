credo — front cover, hero prompt
Era 4, console (late 1980s). Register: tireless. From eras.md
proposal 7, the author's: the classroom, and Credo's outputs on the
board as lessons.

Take 5. Take 4 (`_archived/art/cover-3.jpg`) was accepted, expanded,
cut and stamped, and it is still the source of this cover's lettering:
its lockup was cut out of it as `art/lockup.png` (keyed on its violet
fill, with a ring for the cream outline and the drop shadow). The author
asked for Credo's mark and the title **centred together as one block**,
and a generated lockup cannot give that: the console genre sets its
lockup centred at the foot whatever it is told (exdebug-4, exdebug-5),
and a space asked for beside the title is a space that comes back
filled. So this take asks for **no lettering in the lower part at all**,
and `covers.py stamp --lockup art/lockup.png --mark-beside-lockup`
composites the mark and the lockup as one block, centred exactly.

Take 4's notes, which still hold: three takes have been archived, and between them they settled
one thing: **a decimal fraction written in this prompt comes back
lettered on the face.** Take 1 (carton) returned twenty-seven of them
with leader rules; take 3 (this era, eleven fractions) returned six as a
scale down the left margin. It is not the era — the carton block's own
"technical schematic" was a suspect and console cleared it — and it is
not a missing prohibition: take 1 forbade measurements, dimension lines,
leaders and captions by name, and came back with all four drawn.

So this take carries **no decimal fraction at all**. The reference image
is at the hero's own proportion and already holds every position the old
coordinate list held; giving both was giving the generator two answers,
and it drew the numbers. Placement is the drawing's job here, stated in
words.

The one placement the drawing does not settle is the top of the frame:
take 3 hung the board a sixth of the way down, where the composited mark
lands, so the band above the board is described below and is the thing
to check first on what comes back.

THE HERO IS GENERATED FROM A REFERENCE IMAGE, attached to turn 1:
  art/reference-classroom-4x5.png
the author's drawing of a classroom, cropped to the hero's own 4:5 on
the board's centre. The original, `reference-classroom.png`, 1595×986
landscape, is beside it as the source.

THE BADGE IS NOT IN THIS PROMPT. Credo's own mark is composited
afterwards by `covers.py stamp --mark credo=marks/1-credo.png`, centred
along the top of the window. Nothing here asks the generator for a logo
or a mark: the one time a generator was asked for one it put "Nintendo"
in the seal. The hero's job is to leave the top of the image an even
field so the mark has somewhere to land.

--- turn 1, with art/reference-classroom-4x5.png attached ---

Late-1980s console game box cover art. Painted airbrush illustration with
the slightly stiff, heroic look of North American 8-bit era packaging:
hard airbrush gradients, chunky specular highlights, visible matte
cardboard grain.

Flat cover illustration filling the entire image edge to edge, all of
it in one plane: the four edges of the image are the four edges of the
illustration, and the illustration is all there is — not a product
photograph, not a box or a board: no bevel, no lip, no rim of light, no
edge, no shadow, no surface behind it, no margin of any colour around
it. Proportions 4:5 portrait. The ground of the illustration, edge to
edge, is one tone that reads clearly lighter or darker in value than
Elixir violet (#4B275F).

The attached drawing is the reference for the room, and it is a picture
of a place: a classroom seen from the back of the room in one-point
perspective, with a blackboard on the far wall, an empty chair behind a
teacher's desk, and a tiled floor running toward the viewer. Paint that
same place, in that same perspective, with the blackboard, the desk, the
chair and the floor at the size and in the position the drawing gives
them, in the manner of the first two paragraphs — airbrushed, heroic,
lit — rather than in the drawing's own coloured-pencil shading, outline
weight, colours or framing.

The ceiling and the band of bare far wall above the blackboard together
fill the top quarter of the image, lit, even and calm, with nothing
hanging in them and nothing drawn on them: the board's frame begins
below that quarter, as it does in the drawing.

The light comes from high on the left and falls across the far wall and
the blackboard, so the board is the brightest thing in the room and the
desk and the chair below it stand in the warmer shadow the light leaves.
The walls, the ceiling and the tiled floor are all a warm ochre,
airbrushed from pale at the top of the wall to deep at the foot of the
floor, and that ochre is clearly lighter in value than Elixir violet.
The far wall is bare to the left of the board and to the right of it.

The blackboard is a deep slate green field in a heavy dark frame, wide
and low, the lit half of it paler than the half in shadow. Every chalk
mark on it is opaque and bright, in a plain hand at foreground size,
each stroke carrying a hard specular highlight along its upper edge.

Chalked across the top of the board, a row of five tags, one character
in brackets each, all on one line, evenly spaced across the left two
thirds of the board, reading

  [C]   [D]   [F]   [R]   [W]

and below that row, filling the rest of the board, three lines of chalk
left-aligned under the first tag, one under the other, reading

  Modules should have a @moduledoc tag.
  There should be no calls to dbg/1.
  Avoid long quote blocks.

A chalk rail runs along the foot of the board, carrying a short stub of
chalk toward its left end and a felt eraser toward its right. Those five
tags, those three lines, the chalk and the eraser are everything the
board carries.

The teacher's desk stands where the drawing puts it, solid and
monumental, its top clear; the chair stands between the desk and the
board, turned to face the room, and there is nobody in it, and nobody
anywhere in the room.

Below the desk, the tiled floor runs on toward the viewer to the bottom
edge of the image: the lower quarter of the image is that floor, warm,
evenly lit and calm, its tiles receding in the same one-point
perspective, the desk's shadow falling back across it and nothing
standing on it.

The five chalked tags and the three chalked lines are all the lettering
in the image.

The top left of the artwork is composed as a quiet area: low detail, no
focal element, an even field of tone — the lit ceiling.

Every mark on the face is printed illustration or type, and the marks
named above are all of them.

The reference's own chalked line, its two ceiling fixtures, its wall
clock, its map, its plant, its side table and its flag are not in this
image; the far wall and the ceiling are bare where they stood.

--- turn 2, with the padded canvas from covers.py pad ---

The flat grey border around this picture is blank. Paint it by
continuing the picture outward into it on every side, to the edges —
the lit ceiling continues above, the tiled floor continues below, and
the warm ochre far wall continues to both sides. Keep the picture inside
exactly as it is, and add nothing new: no figure, no object, no
lettering.
