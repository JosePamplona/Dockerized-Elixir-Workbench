# exdebug — six eras

Written from the cartridge README and the library it installs, and
nothing else. What the mechanism does: `ExDebug.console/2` is chained
at any point of a pipeline; there it prints the value passing through,
framed between two ruled lines — a label and the time on the top rule,
the app's name and version on the bottom one — and returns the value
unchanged to the next step. In `:prod` it prints nothing and the value
still passes. A window on a pipe: you see what is going through, and
nothing about the flow changes for having been seen.

## The register

**Deadpan** — the mechanism is documented apparatus and needs no
selling. It shows a value and passes it on; the picture states that.

The term shown is always the same one, at foreground size, and it is
the only lettering the hero carries: `["Lorem", "Ipsum"]`. No
timestamp, no version string — digits below foreground size flip.

## The six heroes

Each is the hero paragraph as it would go into the cover prompt, under
its era's block verbatim. Every position is measured from the edges of
the image; everything sits inward of the bleed (the top sixth, the
bottom tenth, a sixth of the width at each side). Under the colour rule
as it stands, each hero names its ground, and none of them is violet.

### 1 · Early carton, ~1978-82

A technical schematic in two spot colours on cream uncoated board, the
cream continuing to all four edges: one straight pipe drawn in line,
running level across the whole width of the image with its centreline
eleven twentieths of the height down, two flanged joints on it, one at
a third of the width and one at two thirds, each flange a stack of
three ruled lines. Midway between the flanges, centred on the width, an
inspection port: a circle a quarter of the width in diameter, drawn as
a double rim, and inside the circle, set in the second spot colour in
plain typewriter capitals at foreground size, the term
`["Lorem", "Ipsum"]`. One arrowhead on the pipe to the left of the
port and one to the right, both pointing right, so the flow is read as
entering and leaving unchanged. Above the pipe, small hairline leader
lines from the port to the margin, ending in nothing but a short rule —
the callouts of a drawing that has no words on it.

### 2 · Home computer, ~1982-85

A receding grid floor in one hard colour over black, the horizon at
three fifths of the height down and the grid converging on a point
centred on the width. On the floor, a conveyor drawn as two heavy
black-outlined rails running from the left edge to the right edge at
two thirds of the height down, and on it three identical square crates
in one flat colour with heavy black outlines, at a quarter, a half and
three quarters of the width, each a sixth of the width wide. Above the
middle crate, floating, one flat rectangular screen twice the crate's
width and half its height, its lower edge at the crate's top, showing
in geometric slab capitals at foreground size the term
`["Lorem", "Ipsum"]`; a hard-edged beam of the same colour goes from
the screen down onto the middle crate. The left and right crates are
the same crate, untouched.

### 3 · Editorial cover, mid-1980s

An even ground of one pale colour — bone or warm grey — from edge to
edge, and one object on it, centred on the width with its centre nine
twentieths of the height down: a brass-rimmed sight glass, the kind
bolted into a pipe, seen straight on, a third of the width in
diameter, lit from the upper left so the rim catches one crescent of
light and the glass holds one small reflection. Through the glass, set
in a small serif face at foreground size and centred, the single line
`["Lorem", "Ipsum"]`, printed as if it were the thing behind the
glass. Generous ground on every side; the pipe the glass belongs to is
not drawn. A subtle deboss on the rim's circle.

### 4 · Console, late 1980s

A great horizontal pipeline of riveted steel crossing the whole width of
the image, its centreline at eleven twentieths of the height down and
its diameter a quarter of the height, airbrushed with hard gradients
and one chunky specular highlight along its top, on a ground of deep
steel blue that goes to black at the bottom edge. Set into the pipe at
the centre of the width, a round porthole a fifth of the width in
diameter with a bolted rim of eight bolts, and floating in its glass,
glowing, in bold squared capitals at foreground size, the term
`["Lorem", "Ipsum"]`. Standing on a catwalk in front of the pipe, to
the right of the porthole with the top of the head at a third of the
height down, one technician in coveralls and a hard hat, seen from the
side, one hand resting on the pipe and the face turned to the glass,
airbrushed with the stiff, heroic stance of the era — looking, not
touching. The porthole's glow is on the technician's face and on the
rim; the pipe runs off both edges without a break.

### 5 · CD-ROM, early 1990s

A chrome pipe in perspective coming from the lower left corner of the
image and receding to a vanishing point at two fifths of the width and
a third of the height down, over an airbrushed starfield on black with
a perspective grid in one saturated colour on the lower half. One
section of the pipe, at the centre of the width and half the height
down, is clear glass with chrome collars at both ends, and inside the
glass, extruded metallic capitals with deep bevels and rainbow specular
edges spell the term `["Lorem", "Ipsum"]`, the letters catching the
same light as the chrome. One lens flare where the light hits the
glass section's upper collar. The chrome before and after the glass is
the same chrome, unbroken.

### 6 · Big box PC, ~1992-96

An engine room in deep chiaroscuro, oil-painted, detail carried down
into the shadow: a wall of brass gauges, valves and riveted plate
filling the image, all of it dark. Across the middle, a horizontal pipe
of blackened iron, its centreline at half the height down and its
diameter a fifth of the height, and set into it at the centre of the
width one brass-rimmed sight glass a fifth of the width in diameter —
the only light source in the picture. Inside the glass, lit from
within in a warm glow, engraved capitals at foreground size read
`["Lorem", "Ipsum"]`. The glow falls on the brass rim, on the pipe's
rivets to either side and on the nearest gauge faces, and dies into
the dark before it reaches the edges of the image. The ground, where
the light does not reach, is near-black with a green cast.

## The recommendation

**1**, the early carton. The reason is in the cartridge: everything
this mechanism produces is a *printed* thing — a term between two ruled
lines, in a monospaced console, on a page — and the two-colour offset
era is the one entry of the six that prints rather than paints: line,
rule, spot colour, type set by hand. Its schematic pipe with arrows in
and out also says the one fact the deadpan register needs stated, that
the value passes through unchanged, without a figure to say it. The
sight-glass heroes (3, 4, 6) all say "look" well; only the schematic
says "and it keeps going" as a drawing.
