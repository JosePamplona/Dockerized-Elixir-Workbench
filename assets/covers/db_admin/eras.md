# db_admin — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md, and nothing else. The era block of the winner goes into
`cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what follows is the hero paragraph each
era would put in it.

## The need

*You want to look at the database, not type SQL blind into its shell
client.* Before: a shell into the container, then `psql`, `mysql`,
`sqlcmd` or `sqlite3`, `\dt` or its cousin, and a query retyped for
every table. After: a database admin in the browser, on its own port
beside the app's, already open on the project's database — pgAdmin,
phpMyAdmin, Adminer or CloudBeaver.

## The register

**Lucid** — the mechanism lets you see what you had been handling
blind, laid out as it is, and leaves the hands to you. Subtitles
uncover (`SEE WHAT YOU KEEP`). Added for this cartridge: *candid*, the
dashboard's, shows what is happening while it happens; *custodial*,
ecto's, holds what you hand it. Lucid is the third side of the same
store: what it holds, visible at last, rows and all, without reaching
in by touch.

Title `DB ADMIN`. Subtitle `SEE WHAT YOU KEEP`. Badge `4 ADMINS` — the
box's own count, the values of its option — as plain lettering in the
era's own type. No furniture unless the era's own column has one.

The picture's metaphor is the store ecto keeps: a **store of drawers
full of cards**, a table a drawer, a row a card. What the box *is* is
that store seen — its contents in plain view, in order, to someone
standing before it — where before it was a dark cabinet reached into
through a slot. A hero shows **one** store: the four databases are one
at a time, never side by side. No hero draws a real product's mark, an
animal emblem or a known logo; the admins are not pictured as brands.
Card faces carry ruled lines, not writing: digits and words flip below
foreground size. Where there is a person, it is the developer, and
they are *looking*, not rummaging.

The counts that are true: **four** admins; **one** store; its drawers
are tables and its cards rows, any number of them, so the drawers are
a regular grid the prompt counts — the count is the picture's, not the
design's. The strings a hero may carry at foreground size: `DB ADMIN`,
`SEE WHAT YOU KEEP`, `4 ADMINS`. The lockup takes the bottom of the
hero, the badge the top left, and the top right is a quarter-disc of
calm for the seal.

## The recommendation

**1, the early carton.** The box's claim is *seeing inside something
that stays closed*: the database is never opened up, moved or copied —
an admin is a way to see its contents where they are. The cutaway is
the drawing convention that exists for exactly that: it shows the
inside of a sealed thing without taking it apart, and it is the
carton's own idiom — a technical schematic, not a painted scene. The
closed third of the vault with its slot keeps the need's *Before* in
the picture without a word (the shell client was the slot), and the
pulled drawers in the one spot colour are the *After*. The risk is the
era's thin line against a hero that has to read at a shelf's
distance: the green fill on the drawers carries it.

If the developer has to be the picture, **4, the console**: the open
vault and its owner in the doorway is the register drawn literally,
and its four portholes carry the badge's count in the picture.

The shelf as it stands: clustering manual from a home-computer start;
healthcheck2 and dashboard_extras manual from editorial starts;
exdebug console; exdoc, ash and versioning big box; version_manager
home computer; the base cartridges one shared manual front; coveralls,
healthcheck and enhancements starting over. No cover chosen from the
six is early carton or CD-ROM yet; big box already has three, which is
why 6 is not the recommendation.

## The choice

**4, the console** (2026-09-19), the alternative, chosen over the
carton recommendation, with one decision of the author's beside it:
**the four admins' own marks go on the cover as its badge**, in place
of the typeset `4 ADMINS`. Writing the prompt moved three things of the
proposal, and the proposal above is left as it was written:

* **The marks are composited, never generated.** The rule that keeps
  the seal and the overlay out of the prompt (the generator put
  "Nintendo" in a seal it was told to keep free of brands) holds for
  real marks most of all: the prompt names no product, and the four
  marks are the projects' own files, in `marks/` with their sources,
  laid on by `covers.py` as the seal is. The reference the author
  brought, `art/logos.jpg`, was checked against the projects: it drew
  DBeaver's beaver for CloudBeaver, phpMyAdmin's legacy schooner, and
  Adminer's cylinder without its `<?` — so it is a reference, not a
  source.
* **The top fifth of the hero is kept calm across the whole width**,
  dark stone beyond the vault's light: the strip of four marks takes
  its left, the seal its right corner. The flash is not asked for.
* **The portholes stay**, four around the door's wheel, glowing and
  unmarked: the picture keeps the count, the marks say the names.

The door moved to the right of the opening (hinged there, its face at
three-quarters), and the developer stands at the left with her hand on
the rim, since both would not fit the left of a 4:5 frame. No
furniture: the strip of marks is the cover's commercial face already,
and the era's own column (a price sticker, a rental label) would
crowd the same top band.
