# tailwind — six proposals

Written from the cartridge's NEED.md first, then its README and
DESIGN.md, and nothing else. The era block of the winner goes into
`cover.prompt.txt` verbatim; what follows is the hero paragraph each
era would put in it.

## The need

*Your markup was written for Tailwind, and a frozen stylesheet is
styling it.* Before: classes in every template that mean nothing, an
80 kB snapshot that never changes, no theme, no icons. After: the
classes come alive, rebuilt on save; heroicons, daisyUI, a
light/dark/system toggle.

## The register

**Comic** — the mechanism is friendly, and a little silly about it:
the page was dressed all along and only now gets to show it.
Subtitles endear.

Title `TAILWIND`. Subtitle `DRESSED AT LAST`. Badge `CSS/APP.CSS` —
the one source the need names. Accent **daisy pink** or the era's
nearest, unused on the shelf. No furniture unless the era's own
column has one.

The counts that are true: **one** snapshot replaced; **three** theme
positions; **two** sets that come with it, icons and components;
rebuilt on **every** save. The strings a hero may carry at foreground
size, one per hero: `class=`, `app.css`, `data-theme`, `hero-`. Where
something is grey, it is the snapshot; where it is coloured, it is
alive.

## The six heroes

### 1 · Early carton, ~1978-82

A two-colour dressmaker's sheet drawn with a ruler: two identical
outlines of a plain dress side by side, the left one at 0.28 of the
width drawn in the paper's single line only, a dozen small ruled tags
pinned to it on leader lines, each a lettering shape; the right one at
0.72 of the width the same outline **filled** in the second spot
colour, the same tags now drawn as small filled swatches. Between them
at 0.50 of the width and 0.30 of the height, a small three-position
switch drawn in plan. On the left dress's collar tag, at foreground
size in hand-set grotesque: `class=`. Flat paper ground. Lockup along
the bottom fifth, a lot stamp at the top left, top right bare paper.

### 2 · Home computer, ~1982-85

A grid floor to a horizon at 0.45 of the height, and standing on it a
blocky mannequin with a heavy black outline, 0.65 of the height tall,
centred at 0.50 of the width, its left half drawn as outline on the
flat ground only and its right half airbrushed in flat daisy pink and
cyan, the colour boundary a hard vertical line down its middle with
three small flat squares of colour flying in from the right edge
along motion lines. At its feet on the grid a slab three-position
switch, set to the right. Slab lettering `app.css` across the
mannequin's chest at foreground size. Flat empty sky above. Lockup in
slab caps along the bottom, a compatibility strip at the top left, the
top right the empty sky.

### 3 · Editorial cover, mid-1980s

An even ground of daisy pink and one object: a single grey wooden
artist's mannequin, seated, centred at 0.50 of the width and 0.50 of
the height, 0.55 of the height tall, lit from the upper left — wearing
one small, perfectly tailored coat in a saturated blue, the only
colour on it, its seam catching the light. On the coat's lapel, a tiny
tag in small serif at foreground size: `class=`. Nothing else. Small
serif lockup at the bottom, a bookshop price sticker at the top left,
the top right clear pink.

### 4 · Console, late 1980s

An airbrushed tailor, grinning, 0.75 of the height tall with the head
at 0.18 of the height and the centre at 0.40 of the width, whipping a
grey dust sheet off a dress form at 0.70 of the width to reveal a coat
of many bright panels underneath, each panel with a chunky highlight,
the sheet frozen mid-air above at 0.20 of the height. On the wall
behind at 0.85 of the width, three light switches in a column, the
middle one lit. Painted lettering `data-theme` along the dust sheet's
hem at foreground size. Extruded lockup across the bottom fifth; a
rental label at the top right; the top left quadrant the shop's calm
wall.

### 5 · CD-ROM, early 1990s

A chrome mannequin torso, 0.55 of the height tall, centred at 0.50 of
the width and 0.45 of the height over a perspective grid to a
starfield horizon at 0.70 of the height, its left half dull grey metal
and its right half mirror chrome with rainbow specular, the boundary
a hard vertical seam, a lens flare at the seam's top; wrapping onto
the chrome half from the right edge, a ribbon of bevelled metal
carrying a row of small hexagonal icon shapes. Extruded metallic
lettering `TAILWIND` across the chest at foreground size. Chrome
lockup along the bottom, an award flash at the top left, the top
right the starfield.

### 6 · Big box PC, ~1992-96

A tailor's workshop at night in oil, one lamp at the upper left: a
dress form at the centre, 0.70 of the height tall, half of it still
wrapped in a grey dust cloth painted with every fold, the other half
bared and wearing a coat of small bright patches, each patch's weave
detailed down into the shadow; on the bench in front, a bolt of cloth
with its selvedge embroidered at foreground size: `hero-`. Pinned to
the wall, three small painted cards — a sun, a moon, a screen.
Extruded fantasy lettering across the bottom fifth with a hard bevel,
lit by the lamp; a `256 COLORS`-idiom flash at the top right; the top
left the lamp and the dark.

## The recommendation

**2, the home computer.** The need is a before and an after on the
same body — the markup unchanged, the colour arriving — and the
home-computer era can put the boundary on the picture as a hard line
down the middle of one flat figure, with nothing to explain. The
comic register is in the mannequin's pose, not in a tailor.

The shelf as it stands: clustering manual from a home-computer start;
healthcheck2 manual from an editorial start; exdebug console; exdoc
big box; ash big box; coveralls, healthcheck and enhancements starting
over. The home-computer era has no printed cover; live's proposals
recommend it too — if it goes there, 5, the half-chrome torso, says
the same seam in the CD-ROM era, which has none.
